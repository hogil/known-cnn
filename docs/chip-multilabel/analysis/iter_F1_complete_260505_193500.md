## 1. Root-cause diagnosis (the real story behind the per-class table)

The team-lead's per-11-class F1 table shows `bank_boundary+scratch_rot` at F1=0.49 — by far the
worst class. I drilled into `preds_chip.parquet` to find the mechanism.

### Mean per-class probabilities by GT class (T1_LS20 model)

| GT class                  | prob_bb | prob_fork | prob_scratch | prob_sr |
|--------------------------|--------:|----------:|-------------:|--------:|
| bank_boundary             |   0.895 |     0.350 |        0.424 |   0.365 |
| bank_boundary+fork        |   0.841 |     0.427 |        0.445 |   0.383 |
| bank_boundary+scratch     |   0.703 |     0.267 |        0.743 |   0.212 |
| **bank_boundary+scratch_rot** | **0.520** | 0.354 |    0.427 | **0.547** |
| scratch_rot               |   0.288 |     0.338 |        0.211 |   0.770 |
| fork+scratch_rot          |   0.244 |     0.477 |        0.202 |   0.741 |

**Thresholds**: bb=0.54, fork=0.38, scratch=0.58, scratch_rot=0.46.

### The three failure mechanics

1. **Destructive interference between bb and scratch_rot.**
   - `bank_boundary` single → prob_bb = 0.895
   - In `bb+fork` → prob_bb = 0.841 (mild drop, fork doesn't compete)
   - In `bb+scratch` → prob_bb = 0.703 (moderate drop)
   - In **`bb+scratch_rot`** → prob_bb = **0.520** (collapsed *right at threshold 0.54*).
   - Same on the scratch_rot side: 0.770 single → 0.547 in bb+sr.
   - Within the bb+sr group there's an *anti-correlation*: chips with prob_bb<0.54 have mean
     prob_sr=0.591, chips with prob_bb≥0.54 have mean prob_sr=0.495. The model treats bb and sr
     as **mutually exclusive** rather than co-occurring.

2. **Phantom fork in bb+sr.** prob_fork=0.354 in bb+sr (vs 0.267 in bb+scratch). 30.6% of bb+sr
   chips have prob_fork ≥ 0.38 (the fork threshold). This explains the 36 chips that collapsed
   to `fork+scratch_rot` — when bb fails the threshold, fork sneaks in as the second positive.

3. **Single-label CE collapses runner-up scores.** Confirmed by training data census:
   `D:/project/data/wm-811k/classification_chips/` is **single-label only** (bank_boundary=104,
   fork=100, scratch=105, scratch_rot=100). The model has *literally never seen* a chip
   labeled with two classes at training time. The softmax-with-LS=0.20 winner emerged because
   LS spreads runner-up mass — but it can't teach genuine co-occurrence semantics. The combos
   the model handles best (`bb+fork`, `bb+scratch`, `fork+scratch_rot`) are the ones whose
   visual patterns *don't spatially overlap*. `bb+scratch_rot` is the worst because the
   rotated-stripe stamp visually overlaps the bank boundary band most heavily (rotated stripes
   sweep the band region).

### bb+sr error breakdown (108 FN, 160 GT)

| pred class            | count |
|-----------------------|------:|
| scratch_rot single    |    43 |
| fork+scratch_rot      |    36 |
| bank_boundary single  |    15 |
| bank_boundary+fork    |     4 |
| fork single           |     4 |
| scratch               |     3 |
| bank_boundary+scratch |     2 |
| Normal                |     1 |

**62 chips collapsed to a single-class prediction; 46 went to a wrong combo.** The dominant
single is `scratch_rot` (43); among wrong-combos the dominant is `fork+scratch_rot` (36).
Both signal the same root cause: bb failed the threshold.

## 2. Three candidate experiments (priority order)

### **Option 1 (TOP) — Single-label-source CutMix combo synthesis (T7) + retrain**

**Variant**: T7. New variant that, at training time with prob `p_mix=0.5`, takes two single-label
chips of distinct classes from the same batch, performs a 50/50 CutMix-style spatial mix
(half-image swap or rectangular paste, NOT rotation — preserving scratch vs scratch_rot), and
sets the target as the **union of the two single-label one-hot vectors** (i.e., truly multi-hot,
two positives). Loss switches from softmax-CE+LS to **BCE-with-LS=0.20** so multi-hot is
natively supported.

Rationale: the failure mechanism above is "model never saw co-occurrence". CutMix with
multi-hot label propagation is exactly the published fix — `arXiv:2405.13451` (Label
Propagation Strategy for CutMix in Multi-Label Remote Sensing) shows that direct CutMix in
multi-label causes label noise, but for our setup (single-label sources → guaranteed-correct
multi-hot target) the label is exactly right. `arXiv:2110.00476` (ResNet strikes back, timm)
validates CutMix+BCE specifically. SpliceMix `arXiv:2311.15200` explicitly motivates
single→multi label augmentation.

**CLI sketch** (proposes new flags + new variant id):
```
python -m chip_multilabel._train_chip_variant \
    --variant T7_cutmix_bce \
    --epochs 8 --lr 1e-4 --ls 0.20 \
    --cutmix-p 0.5 --cutmix-mode rectangular \
    --cutmix-disallow-rotated-pair true \
    --tag T7_LS20_cutmix50_ep8 --seed 42
```

**Why specifically targets bb+sr**: with `cutmix-p=0.5` and 4 classes, expected combo
frequency is uniform over `{bb,fork,scratch,sr} × {bb,fork,scratch,sr}` minus same-class.
That gives bb+sr training samples roughly = `0.5 × N_train × 2/12 ≈ 0.083×N_train` per
epoch. The model learns the *exact* spatial overlap pattern that currently breaks it.

**Implementation cost**: ~80 LOC in `_train_chip_variant.py` (CutMix collator + BCE-with-LS
loss path). One new variant + 3 flags. ~1 day to ship.

**Expected gain**:
- Optimistic: macro_f1 0.95+, top1_11 0.88+ (bb+sr F1 0.49 → 0.80, fork+scratch F1 0.75 → 0.85
  by similar mechanism), all classes lift a little.
- Pessimistic: 0.90 / 0.82 (gain on bb+sr only ~F1 0.65, no gain elsewhere because BCE switch
  costs us some single-class margin — same risk that killed T5 BCE at F1=0.80).
- Middle: 0.93 / 0.85 (~+0.005 macro, but bb+sr F1 0.49 → 0.72 — meaningful per-class).

**Risk**: BCE without strong LS killed T5/T6 last time. **Mitigation**: keep LS=0.20 (winner)
inside the BCE loss as soft target relaxation. Also gate the experiment with `cutmix-p` sweep
{0.0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7} — if 0.0 (BCE+LS only, no cutmix) is already worse than 0.9268 we know
it's the loss switch not cutmix. If 0.3/0.5/0.7 monotonically beat 0.0 we know cutmix is the
mover.

**Citations**: `arXiv:2405.13451`, `arXiv:2311.15200` (SpliceMix), `arXiv:2110.00476` (ResNet
strikes back), `arXiv:1905.04899` (CutMix original).

### **Option 2 — Pair-aware threshold (I11) on the existing T1_LS20_ep8 model**

**Variant**: I11 — *no retraining*. New inference variant that lowers the bb and sr thresholds
**conditionally**, when the *other* class is also active. Concretely: keep T1's per-class
thresholds (bb=0.54, sr=0.46) for the standard pass. Then run a second pass: if
`prob_bb ≥ 0.40 AND prob_sr ≥ 0.40 AND max(prob_fork, prob_scratch) < 0.45`, declare both bb
and sr active regardless of individual threshold. The 0.40/0.40 corner is exactly where bb+sr
chips cluster (mean 0.520 / 0.547) but Normal+other negatives don't.

Rationale: `arXiv:2404.16193` (Improving Multi-label Recognition using Class Co-Occurrence
Probabilities) — joint probability refinement using conditional pair priors. We have empirical
support: among the 87 bb+sr chips with prob_bb<0.54, mean prob_sr is **0.591** (well above
0.46). A conditional pair gate cleanly catches the missed combo.

**CLI sketch**: add I11 to `chip_multilabel/inference_variants.py` (~30 LOC). Re-run
inference matrix on existing predictions cache — no GPU needed, ~5 min.

**Expected gain**:
- Optimistic: bb+sr recall 0.32 → 0.65, F1 0.49 → 0.70, macro_f1 0.93 → 0.945, top1_11 0.84 → 0.86.
- Pessimistic: bb+sr recall +0.10, macro_f1 +0.005. Gate triggers FP on Normal — net neutral.

**Risk**: Inference-only ceilings (we saw +0.006 from I10). But **stacks orthogonally with
Option 1** — running I11 on top of T7-trained model would compound. Cheap to test first.

**Citations**: `arXiv:2404.16193`.

**Why second, not first**: previous lesson (iter 5) — once the model is well-trained,
inference tricks plateau. Training-side fixes the underlying logit distribution; inference-side
patches symptoms. But this one is so cheap to test it should run *before* Option 1 finishes,
to set a "free" upper bound for the inference ceiling.

---

### **Option 3 — Class-balanced focal-style negative weighting + targeted oversampling (T8)**

**Variant**: T8. Stay with CE+LS=0.20 (winner architecture), but during training:
- (a) **Oversample**: weighted `WeightedRandomSampler` with weights [1, 1.5, 1, 2.0] for
  [bb, fork, scratch, scratch_rot] — boost scratch_rot×2 (the diffuse-prior class from
  04_error_analysis.md) and fork×1.5 (the over-firing class).
- (b) **Add 8 hand-crafted bb+sr training chips** by manually compositing the existing
  single-label sources via min-blend (the same composition method used to build the eval
  set, see `chip_multilabel/gen_eval_set.py`). This is ~5 min of work and gives the model
  explicit grounding for the worst combo. Tag train labels as multi-hot. Switch to BCE+LS=0.20
  loss head for these 8 (or use a multi-hot CE).

Rationale: Cheap, surgical fix. The 327 single-label train chips currently never demonstrate
the specific bb+sr overlap geometry. Ten well-chosen synthetic combos should be enough for the
TAPT backbone to generalize (transfer learning literature consistently shows 8-32 examples
suffice for new compositional concepts). `arXiv:2401.16549` survey notes oversampling +
synthetic minority generation as the dominant intervention for imbalanced multi-label.

**CLI sketch**:
```
python -m chip_multilabel._train_chip_variant \
    --variant T8_targeted \
    --epochs 8 --lr 1e-4 --ls 0.20 \
    --sampler weighted --class-weights 1,1.5,1,2.0 \
    --extra-train-data D:/project/data/wm-811k/classification_chips/_synthetic_combo \
    --tag T8_LS20_combo8_sr2x_ep8
```

**Expected gain**:
- Optimistic: bb+sr F1 0.49 → 0.75, macro_f1 0.94, top1_11 0.86. Cheaper but smaller than T7.
- Pessimistic: macro_f1 0.92 (regression). 8 synthetic chips overfit, model memorizes them,
  doesn't generalize to eval combos which are differently composed.

**Risk**: small synthetic set could overfit. Mitigation: use *different* source chips than
the eval set generator uses (eval generator is deterministic with seed; pick non-overlapping
sources). **Don't use this path if Option 1 works** — Option 1 is more general.

**Citations**: `arXiv:2401.16549` (Deep Learning for Multi-Label Learning survey),
`arXiv:2403.09598` (Mixture of Mixups for rare-combo classes).

## 3. What I deliberately did NOT recommend

- **Per-instance margin threshold (team-lead's option f)**: equivalent to I7's joint coord
  descent which is already the winner. No fundamental change.
- **ASL γ_neg sweep (option d)**: T4 ASL regressed -0.078 in iter 4 *under the same
  protocol*. Even with γ sweep, the loss is fundamentally wrong for our 327-chip TAPT setup
  — it punishes negatives but our negative shortage isn't the problem; the problem is the
  data has no co-occurrence supervision. ASL doesn't fix that.
- **Hard negative mining for bank+scratch_rot (option e)**: redundant with Option 3 (which
  is implicitly hard-negative-aware via the synthetic combo + sampling). Hard negatives
  alone don't teach co-occurrence.
- **Backbone unfreeze schedule (option g)**: TAPT backbone is already producing good
  per-class features (singles all have ≥0.77 prob). Issue is at the *head* level — joint
  decoding, not feature extraction. Unfreezing risks destabilizing.
- **Phase F-style EMA / drop_path tweaks**: F2 EMA(0.95) is in flight; if it lifts macro_f1
  above 0.93 we have a new winner before any of the above. But none of these structural
  tweaks address the bb+sr root cause — they're orthogonal.

## 4. Recommended priority

1. **Run Option 2 (I11 pair-aware threshold) first** — ~5 min compute, no retrain, free
   upper bound. If it reaches 0.94+ it stacks with everything else.
2. **Run Option 1 (T7 CutMix+BCE+LS) second** — biggest expected gain, ~6 min training
   per cell. Sweep `cutmix-p ∈ {0.0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7}` to disentangle BCE-switch vs CutMix
   effect (~25 min total).
3. **Option 3 (T8 targeted oversampling + 8 synthetic combos)** as a fallback if Option 1
   regresses — cheaper variant of the same idea.

Current best 0.9268 → **target 0.95+ macro_f1 / 0.88+ top1_11 with Option 1**.
