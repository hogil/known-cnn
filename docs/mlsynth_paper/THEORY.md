# Theory: Superposition Domains, Label Fidelity, and FAR Guarantees

Draft v0.1 — formalization of the paper's three mechanisms. Proof sketches;
tightening TODOs marked. All empirical constants refer to measured results in
`docs/superpowers/multilabel_synth_RESULTS.md`.

## 1 Setup

K classes; observation x in [0,1]^d (pixels); target y in {0,1}^K multi-hot.
Training data: singles only — samples (x, e_k) with one positive. Evaluation:
real multi-label samples (>=2 positives) and real all-negative normals.
A synthesis operator T maps two singles to a labeled pair sample:
T((x_a, a), (x_b, b)) = (x~, {a, b}).

## 2 Superposition domains and consistency of overlay

**Definition 1 (max-superposition domain).** A domain is a max-superposition
domain if the observation of co-occurring sources equals the pixelwise max of
their solo observations: x_{a,b} = x_a v x_b (up to noise). Equivalently, the
class evidence of k survives composition: evidence is never *replaced*, only
*joined*.

Examples (measured): wafer maps (defect die intensity > normal die > bg —
co-occurring defects union their die sets: MixedWM38); chip palette maps
(min-blend, the dual order); MNIST ink (approx.). Counterexample: natural RGB
scenes — opaque objects OCCLUDE (replace pixels) and interact via
lighting/context, so composition is not a lattice join. This single
definition predicts both our wins (wafer/chip/MNIST) and our boundary (VOC/
COCO): the aware/blind distinction reduces to whether the domain supplies
the join for free.

**Proposition 1 (consistency).** In a max-superposition domain, if real
k-mixes are generated by independently drawing sources and joining them,
then overlay synthesis x~ = x_a v x_b with hard label {a,b} samples EXACTLY
the real 2-mix distribution. Consequently (i) the Bayes-optimal classifier on
synthetic data equals the Bayes-optimal classifier on real mixes, and (ii)
because v is associative and commutative, a model trained on synthesized
pairs faces test k-mixes (k>2) that are joins of pair-joins — the synthesis
family is closed under order. Sketch: distributional identity is immediate
from the generative assumption; closure from associativity.

**Theorem 1 (excess risk of blind synthesis).**
Fix a bounded per-example loss ell(f(x), y) in [0, B]. Write the real-mix
law as D_real = pi_real . K_real and the synthetic law as D_syn = pi_syn .
K_syn, where pi is the distribution over active label subsets S and K(. | S)
is the conditional law of the observation given S. Let
f_syn = argmin_f R_syn(f) be the population risk minimizer on synthetic data
and f_star = argmin_f R_real(f) the oracle. Then

    R_real(f_syn) - R_real(f_star)  <=  2 B . TV(D_real, D_syn)
                                    <=  2 B ( TV(pi_real, pi_syn)
                                             + E_{S ~ pi_syn} TV( K_real(.|S), K_syn(.|S) ) ).

*Proof.* Add and subtract R_syn: the excess risk equals
[R_real(f_syn) - R_syn(f_syn)] + [R_syn(f_syn) - R_syn(f_star)] +
[R_syn(f_star) - R_real(f_star)]. The middle bracket is <= 0 since f_syn
minimizes R_syn. Each outer bracket is a difference of expectations of a
function valued in [0, B] under D_real vs D_syn, hence <= B . TV(D_real,
D_syn). This gives the first line. The chain rule for total variation of a
mixture, TV(pi.K, pi'.K') <= TV(pi, pi') + E_{S ~ pi'} TV(K(.|S), K'(.|S)),
gives the second. QED.

The bound splits the gap to the oracle into exactly two named terms, and a
third appears only outside superposition domains:

1. **Support term** TV(pi_real, pi_syn): the oracle knows which label
   combinations actually co-occur; single-label data cannot supply this. It
   vanishes iff synthesis matches the real co-occurrence prior (or eval is
   restricted to the covered support). This is the sparse-combination-support
   effect measured in Sec. 5 (12/56 real 3-combos): the order-extrapolation
   gap IS this term.
2. **Independence term** E TV(K_real, K_syn) under the SAME join operator:
   real sources may be placed with correlation / physical interaction while
   synthesis joins independent draws. It vanishes when co-occurring sources
   are conditionally independent given S. On WM38 it is the small residual
   oracle gap.

**Corollary 1 (oracle has no information advantage).** In a max-superposition
domain (Def. 1) with conditionally independent sources, K_real(.|S) =
K_syn(.|S) for every S, so if synthesis also matches the support
(pi_syn = pi_real) then TV(D_real, D_syn) = 0 and R_real(f_syn) = R_real(f_star):
blind synthesis is Bayes-equivalent to full multi-label supervision. Label-,
normal-, and location-annotation are then statistically superfluous.

**Corollary 2 (the boundary, without running photos).** If the domain is NOT
a superposition domain — the real conditional K_real(.|S) is generated by an
operator other than the blind join (e.g. opaque occlusion, which *replaces*
rather than *joins* pixels) — then a third, irreducible term
inf_{pi_syn} TV(D_real, D_syn) >= min_S TV( K_real(.|S), join_{c in S} P_c ) > 0
survives no matter how synthesis chooses pi_syn. The excess risk is bounded
away from zero and the oracle retains a genuine advantage. Natural RGB
(VOC/COCO) is exactly this case; the boundary is a *theorem*, not an
empirical accident, so it need not be re-established on every photo dataset.

Fidelity (Sec. 3) enters when the join itself loses evidence (survival S < 1):
the effective conditional is a noisy join and the independence term inherits
an O(1 - phi) contribution, linking Theorem 1 to Proposition 2.

## 3 Label fidelity bounds the label noise of synthesis

**Definition 2 (survival / fidelity).** For operator T and source i, the
survival S_i in x~ is the fraction of class-i evidence preserved (measured:
ink pixels for MNIST, defect dies for WM38, visible area for VOC). The
fidelity of T is phi(T) = E[min(S_a, S_b)], and its catastrophe rate at
detectability threshold s0 is rho(T) = P(min(S_a, S_b) < s0).

Measured (mean survival / rho at s0=0.15):

| operator        | MNIST        | WM38          | VOC (area)    |
|-----------------|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| overlay         | 0.70-1.00 / 0| 1.000 / 0     | (n/a)         |
| copy-paste      | 0.32 / 0.31  | -             | 0.795 / 0     |
| cutmix (common) | 0.10-0.33 / .21-.71 | 0.579 / 0 | 0.250 / 0  |
| complement g9n3 | ~0.49 / 0    | 0.527 / 0     | -             |
| mixup           | (contrast /2)| 0.236 / -     | 0.250 / 0.30  |

**Proposition 2 (fidelity -> risk).** If class-k evidence below s0 is
undetectable, synthesis with catastrophe rate rho produces false-positive
labels at rate rho for the destroyed source: the training distribution has
class-conditional positive-label noise rho. Under standard conditions for
learning with class-conditional label noise (cf. Natarajan et al., 2013),
the achievable recall of the risk minimizer degrades by O(rho), and the
attainable bit-F1 is monotone decreasing in rho and increasing in phi.
Prediction: downstream ranking follows fidelity ranking. Verified on three
dataset families (MNIST, WM38, VOC): survival ordering = bit-F1 ordering in
all three (9/9 pairwise orderings correct).
**Partial survival (S in (s0,1)): margin-shrinkage argument.** Survival does
not merely gate detectability; it scales the evidence margin. If a clean
single of class k induces logit margin m_k at the Bayes-optimal predictor,
a synthesized sample whose class-k evidence survives at rate S contributes
class-k evidence with margin ~ S * m_k (evidence is sub-additive in the
preserved fraction; exactly proportional when evidence is a sum of
independent per-pixel/per-term contributions). Consequences:
(i) the BCE risk minimizer's confidence on bit k is monotone increasing in
S — training on partially-surviving positives teaches calibrated but LOWER
confidence; (ii) any thresholded metric (bit-F1 at 0.5, exact-match) fails
exactly when S * m_k falls below the threshold margin, while ranking metrics
(mAP) degrade more slowly — matching the observed pattern that mAP washes
out operator differences that bit-F1 exposes.

This separates the two failure modes qualitatively:
- **Mixup = uniform margin shrinkage.** Every sample survives at S ~ 1/2 in
  shared-coordinate spaces (pixel ghosting): no label is false, but ALL
  positive margins are halved -> systematically under-confident positives
  (measured: WM38 mixup pos_prob 0.37 vs overlay 0.60) with low FAR — a
  calibration failure, not a label failure.
- **CutMix = bimodal catastrophe.** Survival is near 1 for some samples and
  near 0 for others (rectangle either misses or covers the object): margins
  are intact for survivors but a rho-fraction of labels are FALSE positives
  -> confident wrong supervision -> inflated false alarms (measured: WM38
  cutmix NORMAL FAR up to 1.000) at moderate F1 — a label-noise failure.
- **Text vec-average = no shrinkage.** With (near-)disjoint evidence
  coordinates, averaging halves magnitudes but TF-IDF-normalized linear
  models are scale-robust per coordinate; effective S ~ 1 — which is why the
  mixup-analog WINS in text (0.402 vs concat 0.359). The operator is not the
  invariant; preserved evidence is.

## 4 Distribution-free FAR control by conformal rejection

**Proposition 3 (split-conformal FAR guarantee).** Let C = {c_1..c_n} be
max-bit-probabilities of a calibration set of NORMAL (all-negative) samples,
and set tau = the ceil((n+1)(1-alpha))/n empirical quantile of C. If test
normals are exchangeable with the calibration normals, then
P(alarm on a test normal) = P(max-prob >= tau) <= alpha.
Proof: standard split-conformal argument (rank exchangeability).

Two regimes:
- Calibrate on SYNTHETIC normals (defect-erased singles): guarantee holds for
  synthetic-normal exchangeability; transfer to real normals adds a shift
  term TV(P_syn-normal, P_real-normal). Empirically the transfer holds:
  tau=0.9 gives measured real-normal FAR 0.000 (three models, 1000 real
  normals), i.e., the shift term is small in this domain.
- If a small real-normal calibration set exists (n=100 suffices for
  alpha=0.05 with slack), the guarantee is assumption-light and fully
  distribution-free.

This upgrades stage-3 rejection from a tuned threshold to a finite-sample
guaranteed FAR controller, at measured coverage cost 2-8% of mixed samples.

## 5 What the theory predicts (falsifiable summary)

1. In superposition domains, overlay-synthesis models approach the oracle on
   real mixes (P1) — observed: WM38 0.858/0.773 vs oracle 0.863.
2. Pair-trained models extrapolate to higher-order mixes (P1 closure) —
   VERDICT: partial. Bit-level detection extrapolates (2/3/4-mix bitF1
   0.784/0.671/0.628); joint prediction does not (exact 0.699/0.143/0.000),
   and naive higher-order synthesis hurts. Refinement: real higher-order
   mixes are drawn from a SPARSE combination support (12/56 3-combos, 4/70
   4-combos exist), violating the independence assumption. The delta term of
   P1 is precisely the oracle's knowledge of the combination support — the
   one thing single-label data cannot supply. This sharpens, not breaks, the
   theory: closure holds at the evidence (bit) level where v is genuinely
   associative; it fails at the joint level where co-occurrence is
   structured.
3. Operator ranking follows measured fidelity (P2) — confirmed 3/3 families.
4. Outside superposition domains (natural RGB), blind joins fail and
   content-aware placement (masks) is required — now a THEOREM (Corollary 2:
   a non-join conditional forces an irreducible TV floor, so the oracle keeps
   an advantage), consistent with the observed VOC/COCO boundary. The photo
   result is a confirmation of the corollary, not a required benchmark.
5. Conformal tau bounds FAR at alpha given exchangeability (P3) — VERDICT:
   verified in the real-calibration regime (n=500 known-good samples:
   alpha=0.05 -> realized 0.040; alpha=0.01 -> 0.006; coverage >= 99.5%).
   Synthetic-normal calibration fails (realized FAR 0.97 at tau 0.045):
   training on those synthetic normals collapses their scores, so the
   score-space shift to real normals is large — exchangeability is the
   binding assumption. Practically benign: known-good normals require no
   defect annotation, so the guarantee costs zero labeling expertise.
