# Theory: Blind-Combination Domains, the Operator-Match Principle, Label Fidelity, and FAR Guarantees

Draft v0.1 — formalization of the paper's three mechanisms. Proof sketches;
tightening TODOs marked. All empirical constants refer to measured results in
`docs/superpowers/multilabel_synth_RESULTS.md`.

## 1 Setup

K classes; observation x in [0,1]^d (pixels); target y in {0,1}^K multi-hot.
Training data: singles only — samples (x, e_k) with one positive. Evaluation:
real multi-label samples (>=2 positives) and real all-negative normals.
A synthesis operator T maps two singles to a labeled pair sample:
T((x_a, a), (x_b, b)) = (x~, {a, b}).

## 2 Blind-combination domains and the operator-match principle

**Definition 1 (blind-combination domain; fidelity-maximizing operator).** A
domain is a *blind-combination* domain if the observation of co-occurring
sources equals a known content-blind operator T_dom applied to their solo
observations: x_{a,b} = T_dom(x_a, x_b) (up to noise). T_dom is the domain's
*fidelity-maximizing combination operator* (the operator that best preserves each
source's evidence), and it is domain-specific: on *superposition-structured*
domains — inked digits (pixelwise max v), wafer maps (whole-image max-union of
defect dies), and audio (physical waveform summation) — it is a summation/union
join; on disjoint-vocabulary text it is coordinate averaging. Equivalently, the
class evidence of each source survives composition: evidence is never *replaced*,
only *combined* under T_dom. Counterexample: natural RGB scenes — opaque objects
OCCLUDE (replace pixels) and interact via lighting/context, so no fixed
content-blind operator reproduces composition. This single definition predicts both
our wins (wafer/chip/MNIST/audio/text) and our boundary (VOC/COCO): the aware/blind
distinction reduces to whether the domain supplies a content-blind
evidence-preserving operator for free.

*Reconciliation (density-shift refutation, final).* An earlier draft argued wafer
maps combine by a *die-budget partition* (favoring a complement operator over
summation), on the grounds that whole-image max-union is over-dense (defect-die
fraction 0.50 vs real 0.31). A decisive **density-shift stress test refuted this**
(Sec. 5): max-union beats the density-matching complement (FCM-PM) on *every*
density stratum and mix order, so over-density is empirically harmless. Wafer maps
are therefore grouped with inked digits and audio as *superposition-structured*
domains where summation/union is the fidelity-maximizing operator, and under the
binary encoding max-union coincides with Shin et al. (2022) Summation Mixup — the
closest prior work, and the empirical winner. We claim no operator novelty on
wafer; the operator is *selected* by label fidelity, made precise by the
operator-match theorem below (which is honestly a one-directional upper bound, not
a superiority proof).

**Proposition 1 (consistency under the matched operator).** In a
blind-combination domain with fidelity-maximizing operator T_dom, if real k-mixes
are generated by independently drawing sources and combining them under T_dom, then
synthesis x~ = T(x_a, x_b) with hard label {a,b} samples EXACTLY the real 2-mix
distribution iff the synthesis operator matches the domain, T = T_dom.
Consequently, under T = T_dom, (i) the Bayes-optimal classifier on synthetic
data equals the Bayes-optimal classifier on real mixes, and (ii) if T_dom is
associative and commutative (as pixelwise max and waveform summation are), a
model trained on synthesized pairs faces test k-mixes (k>2) that are
combinations of pair-combinations — the synthesis family is closed under order.
A MISMATCHED operator (T != T_dom) instead shifts the observation law: e.g.
averaging (Mixup) on a superposition-structured domain ghosts both sources to half
contrast, so the synthetic and real 2-mix laws differ and Proposition 1 fails —
precisely the distributional shift the excess-risk *upper bound* charges for
(Theorem 1). Note this shift bounds the excess risk only from above and is
one-directional: a summary-statistic gap (e.g. max-union's over-density on WM38)
lower-bounds TV but need not imply larger real risk — empirically max-union is in
fact the best wafer operator (Sec. 5). Sketch: distributional identity is immediate
from the generative assumption when T = T_dom; closure from associativity of T_dom.

**Theorem 1 (excess risk of blind synthesis, operator-parametrized).**
Fix a bounded per-example loss ell(f(x), y) in [0, B] and a hypothesis class F.
Fix a content-blind synthesis operator T; applying T to INDEPENDENT single
sources induces the synthetic mixed-law D_syn^T = pi_syn^T . K_syn^T, where pi
is the distribution over active label subsets S and K(. | S) is the conditional
law of the observation given S. Write the real-mix law as
D_real = pi_real . K_real. Let f_syn^T = argmin_{f in F} R_syn^T(f) be the
population risk minimizer on operator-T synthetic data and
f_star = argmin_{f in F} R_real(f) the equal-class oracle. Then

    R_real(f_syn^T) - R_real(f_star)  <=  2 B . TV(D_real, D_syn^T)
                                      <=  2 B ( TV(pi_real, pi_syn^T)
                                               + E_{S ~ pi_syn^T} TV( K_real(.|S), K_syn^T(.|S) ) ).

*Proof.* Add and subtract R_syn^T: the excess risk equals
[R_real(f_syn^T) - R_syn^T(f_syn^T)] + [R_syn^T(f_syn^T) - R_syn^T(f_star)] +
[R_syn^T(f_star) - R_real(f_star)]. The middle bracket is <= 0 since f_syn^T
minimizes R_syn^T. Each outer bracket is a difference of expectations of a
function valued in [0, B] under D_real vs D_syn^T, hence
<= B . TV(D_real, D_syn^T). This gives the first line. The chain rule for total
variation of a mixture, TV(pi.K, pi'.K') <= TV(pi, pi') + E_{S ~ pi'} TV(K(.|S),
K'(.|S)), gives the second. QED. (Assumptions: bounded loss in [0, B],
independent-source synthesis, and a single hypothesis class F shared by all
arms — the shift term is class-independent, so the bound applies uniformly over
F and over the choice of T.)

The bound separates two sources of distribution shift. Departures from the
superposition model are included in the conditional-mismatch term:

1. **Support term** TV(pi_real, pi_syn): the oracle knows which label
   combinations actually co-occur; single-label data cannot supply this. It
   vanishes iff synthesis matches the real co-occurrence prior (or eval is
   restricted to the covered support). This is the sparse-combination-support
   effect measured in Sec. 5 (12/56 real 3-combos): the order-extrapolation
   gap IS this term.
2. **Independence term** E TV(K_real, K_syn) under the SAME join operator:
   real sources may be placed with correlation / physical interaction while
   synthesis joins independent draws. It vanishes when co-occurring sources
   are conditionally independent given S. On WM38 it is a plausible
   contributor to the residual oracle gap; the experiments do not identify
   this TV term directly.

**Theorem 1' (operator match).** Theorem 1 holds for EVERY content-blind
operator T. The excess risk is nonnegative (f_star minimizes R_real) and its
certificate depends on T only through TV(D_real, D_syn^T), so the operator
carrying the tightest guarantee is

    T* = argmin_T  TV(D_real, D_syn^T),

the operator whose synthetic-mix law is closest in total variation to the
real-mix law. If T reproduces the domain's fidelity-maximizing operator T_dom
(Def. 1) on conditionally independent sources with matched subset priors, then
TV(D_real, D_syn^{T_dom}) = 0 (Corollary 1) and T* attains the GLOBAL minimum of
the *certificate* (the upper bound). Hence "match the domain's evidence-preserving
combination law" is the minimizer of the excess-risk *bound*. **This is a statement
about the upper bound, not the risk itself, and is honestly one-directional**: a
smaller TV gives a tighter guarantee, but a larger TV does not prove a larger real
risk (Corollary 2). Empirically the fidelity-maximizing operator (summation on
wafer/digits/audio, averaging on text) does win, and on WM38 it coincides with Shin
et al. (2022) Summation Mixup — but the theorem certifies faithfulness, not
optimality, and the density-shift result (Sec. 5) confirms the over-dense operator
is not penalized. Assumptions as in Theorem 1 (bounded loss, independent-source
synthesis, shared F).

**Corollary 1a (density mismatch is a certified TV lower bound).** TV is not
directly observable, but for any measurable summary statistic g the
data-processing inequality gives

    TV(D_real, D_syn^T)  >=  TV( g#D_real, g#D_syn^T ),

so any statistic on which the real and synthetic laws visibly disagree
LOWER-BOUNDS the operator's excess-risk certificate. On WM38 take
g(x) = defect-die fraction. Measured: 91% of max-union synthetics exceed the
real-2-mix 95th percentile, so with the event A = { g > q95_real }
(P_real(A) = 0.05 by definition of the 95th percentile),

    TV(D_real, D_syn^{max-union})  >=  |P_syn(A) - P_real(A)|  =  0.91 - 0.05  =  0.86,

making the guarantee vacuous: 2B . 0.86 = 1.72B exceeds the trivial B ceiling on
excess risk, so the bound guarantees NOTHING for max-union. The full-cover
complement matches the real density (0.293 vs 0.305), so this witness is ~0 and
its certificate stays non-vacuous. **This is a fact about the upper bound only, and
we do NOT use it as a preference argument.** The bound being vacuous means the
certificate is uninformative for max-union — NOT that max-union has a large real
risk. Density mismatch is a measurable lower-bound proxy for the TV in Theorem 1's
*upper* bound; it is retained here only as a general illustration of the bound's
looseness.

*One-directionality and the density-shift refutation (honest caveat).* A large TV
WEAKENS the upper-bound guarantee; it does not by itself prove a strictly larger
real risk (Corollary 2: a positive TV need not imply a positive
classification-risk gap). Empirically the reverse holds: max-union attains the
HIGHEST bit-F1 (0.80) at the lowest FAR (0.010), and a **density-shift stress test
(Sec. 5) shows its over-density is harmless** — it wins on every density stratum and
mix order. We therefore do NOT prefer the density-matching complement on the
strength of this corollary; on WM38 the best content-blind operator is max-union
(= Shin 2022 Summation Mixup), and we report it as such. The corollary stands only
as a general demonstration that a measurable distributional gap lower-bounds the
(one-directional) TV certificate.

**Corollary 1b (cross-regime operator selection).** Because T* = argmin_T
TV(D_real, D_syn^T) depends on D_real, the SAME criterion selects the appropriate
operator per domain. On superposition-structured domains — wafer maps, inked
digits, and audio — the fidelity-maximizing operator is summation/union: on WM38
max-union (= Shin 2022 Summation Mixup) is the empirical winner (bit-F1 0.80), and
on FSD50K physical waveform summation wins (0.433 vs 0.27-0.31). On
disjoint-coordinate text the maximizer flips to coordinate averaging (measured:
vec-average beats concat). The measured selections (wafer / digits / audio ->
summation, text -> averaging) are one criterion instantiated on different D_real,
not a per-domain rule set — the operator, not any fixed operation, is what the
criterion selects. (The partition complement matches wafer density but does not win
there; density-matching is a modeling property, not a superiority argument — Sec. 5
density-shift test.)

**Corollary 1 (no population-risk advantage under matched laws).** In a
blind-combination domain (Def. 1) with conditionally independent sources, using
the domain's fidelity-maximizing operator T = T_dom gives K_real(.|S) = K_syn^T(.|S) for every
evaluated subset S. If synthesis also matches the subset prior
(pi_syn^T = pi_real), then TV(D_real, D_syn^T) = 0 and
R_real(f_syn^T) = R_real(f_star). Blind synthesis is therefore risk-equivalent
to full multi-label supervision within F. If all-negative samples are part of
the evaluation law, this statement includes them only when S=empty is also
synthesized with the correct conditional law.

**Corollary 2 (limit of the zero-shift guarantee).** Assume matched subset
priors. Because label subsets form disjoint blocks of the joint law,

    TV(D_real, D_syn)
      = E_{S ~ pi_real} TV(K_real(.|S), K_syn(.|S)).

Thus, if a set of subsets A has pi_real(A)>0 and its real conditional differs
from the blind-operator conditional K_syn^T, the joint laws are not equal and
Corollary 1 does not apply. Opaque occlusion in natural RGB is a common mechanism that can
create this mismatch; our VOC result is empirical evidence for that boundary.
Importantly, TV>0 alone does NOT prove a strictly positive classification-risk
gap: that stronger conclusion additionally requires the real and synthetic
Bayes decisions to disagree on a positive-mass region. Theorem 1 provides an
upper bound, not a converse lower bound.

Fidelity (Sec. 3) enters when the join itself loses evidence (survival S < 1):
the effective conditional changes, increasing conditional mismatch in general.
A quantitative dependence on 1-phi requires additional assumptions;
Proposition 2 establishes label-content inconsistency directly.

**Theorem 2 (finite-sample).** Let f^ be the empirical risk minimizer over F
trained on m i.i.d. synthetic samples from D_syn, with loss in [0, B], and let
G = {z -> ell(f,z): f in F} be the induced loss class. With probability
>= 1 - delta over the synthetic draw,

    R_real(f^) - R_real(f_star)
        <=  2 B . TV(D_real, D_syn)              (distribution shift, Thm 1)
          + 4 . Rad_m(G)                          (capacity / estimation)
          + 2 B . sqrt( 2 ln(2/delta) / m ).      (concentration)

*Proof.* Decompose R_real(f^) - R_real(f_star) =
[R_real(f^) - R_syn(f^)] + [R_syn(f^) - R_star_syn] + [R_star_syn - R_real(f_star)],
where R_star_syn = inf_{f in F} R_syn(f). Terms 1 and 3 are each
<= B . TV(D_real, D_syn) as in Theorem 1 (the shift is class-independent, so
it applies uniformly over F). The middle term is the excess *synthetic* risk
of ERM, bounded by 4 Rad_m(G) + 2B sqrt(2 ln(2/delta)/m) by the standard
symmetrization + bounded-difference argument. Summing gives the claim. QED.

Reading: the shift term (2B.TV) is the part of this upper bound that does not
shrink with m. If Rad_m(G)->0, then the asymptotic excess risk has limsup at
most 2B.TV(D_real,D_syn); it need not equal that upper bound. Under Corollary 1
the shift term is zero. Full-scale runs can therefore test whether
finite-sample effects remain important, but they do not estimate TV directly.

## 3 Label fidelity bounds the label noise of synthesis

**Definition 2 (survival / fidelity).** For operator T and source i, the
survival S_i in x~ is the fraction of class-i evidence preserved (measured:
ink pixels for MNIST, defect dies for WM38, visible area for VOC). The
fidelity of T is phi(T) = E[min(S_a, S_b)], and its catastrophe rate at
detectability threshold s0 is rho(T) = P(min(S_a, S_b) < s0).

Measured (mean survival / rho at s0=0.15):

| operator        | MNIST        | WM38          | VOC (area)    |
|-----------------|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| overlay         | 0.70-1.00 / 0| 1.000 / 0     | (n/a)         |
| copy-paste      | 0.32 / 0.31  | -             | 0.795 / 0     |
| cutmix (common) | 0.10-0.33 / .21-.71 | 0.579 / 0 | 0.250 / 0  |
| complement g9n3 | ~0.49 / 0    | 0.527 / 0     | -             |
| mixup           | (contrast /2)| 0.236 / -     | 0.250 / 0.30  |

**Proposition 2 (catastrophe implies label-content inconsistency).** Let
h_k(x) indicate whether class-k evidence is detectable, and suppose the
synthesis target retains y_k=1 even when the source is destroyed. Then

    P(h_k(x_tilde)=0 | y_k=1) = rho_k,

so a rho_k fraction of positive targets for k are unsupported by detectable
evidence. This identity is distribution-free. It does not, by itself, imply
monotonic bit-F1: converting inconsistency into a risk or recall bound requires
assumptions on the loss, margins, and hypothesis class. Our testable mechanism
hypothesis is that larger catastrophe rates reduce recall and calibration
under a fixed learner. The measured operator ranking follows that hypothesis
on MNIST, WM38, and VOC (9/9 reported pairwise orderings).

The controlled WM38 attenuation experiment sharpens this statement. Across
five paired model/data splits, counterfactual marginal survival has Spearman
correlation 0.662 with supported-class bit-F1, 0.624 with probability gap, and
-0.656 with NORMAL FAR. Full versus erased evidence changes these quantities
by +0.162, +0.229, and -0.164, with paired bootstrap intervals excluding zero.
However, only 60%, 68%, and 52% of adjacent intervention steps move in the
expected direction. The data therefore support a minimum-sufficient evidence
threshold and catastrophe mechanism, not a globally monotonic performance
law. In particular, f=0.5--0.75 is as good as or better than f=1 in bit-F1.
**Partial survival (S in (s0,1)): margin-shrinkage model.** If a clean single
induces margin m_k and evidence contributions are additive, a sample whose
class-k evidence survives at rate S has margin approximately S*m_k. Under this
explicit model, confidence increases with S and a thresholded decision flips
when S*m_k crosses the decision margin, while ranking metrics may degrade more
slowly. These are model-based predictions, not distribution-free consequences
of Proposition 2.

This separates the two failure modes qualitatively:
- **Mixup = uniform margin shrinkage.** Every sample survives at S ~ 1/2 in
  shared-coordinate spaces (pixel ghosting): no label is false, but ALL
  positive margins are halved -> systematically under-confident positives
  (measured: WM38 mixup pos_prob 0.37 vs overlay 0.60) with low FAR — a
  calibration failure, not a label failure.
- **CutMix = bimodal catastrophe.** Survival is near 1 for some samples and
  near 0 for others (rectangle either misses or covers the object): margins
  are intact for survivors but a rho-fraction of labels are FALSE positives
  -> confident wrong supervision -> inflated false alarms (measured: WM38
  cutmix NORMAL FAR up to 1.000) at moderate F1 — a label-noise failure.
- **Text vec-average = no shrinkage.** With (near-)disjoint evidence
  coordinates, averaging halves magnitudes but TF-IDF-normalized linear
  models are scale-robust per coordinate; effective S ~ 1 — which is why the
  mixup-analog WINS in text (0.402 vs concat 0.359). The operator is not the
  invariant; preserved evidence is.

## 4 Distribution-free FAR control by conformal rejection

**Proposition 3 (split-conformal FAR guarantee).** Let C = {c_1..c_n} be
max-bit-probabilities of a calibration set of NORMAL (all-negative) samples.
Let k=ceil((n+1)(1-alpha)) and tau be the k-th order statistic (tau=infinity
when k>n). If test normals are exchangeable with the calibration normals, then

    P(max-prob_test > tau) <= alpha.

This is a marginal finite-sample guarantee from rank exchangeability. Using
">=" instead requires continuous scores/no ties or randomized tie-breaking.

Two regimes:
- Calibrate on SYNTHETIC normals (defect-erased singles): the guarantee holds
  only for synthetic-normal exchangeability; transfer to real normals adds a
  shift term TV(P_syn-normal, P_real-normal). It fails in our measured score
  space (realized real-normal FAR 0.97 at tau=0.045), so we do not claim a
  real-normal guarantee from synthetic calibration.
- If a real-normal calibration set exists, the guarantee is distribution-free
  conditional on exchangeability. For alpha=0.05, n>=19 makes the conformal
  quantile non-vacuous; n=100 provides roughly 0.01 rank resolution.

This upgrades stage-3 rejection from a tuned threshold to a finite-sample
marginal FAR controller under exchangeability, at measured coverage cost 2-8%
of mixed samples.

## 5 What the theory predicts (falsifiable summary)

1. In blind-combination domains, the fidelity-maximizing operator should recover
   most of the oracle on real mixes — observed on WM38: the winning summation
   operator (max-union = Shin 2022) reaches strict bit-F1 0.80 vs equal-condition
   oracle 0.974+-0.019 (~82% recovery); the nonzero gap shows the theorem's
   matching assumptions (independence of co-occurring sources) are not exact.
   HONEST NOTE on the bound: max-union is over-dense (0.50 vs real 0.31), so its
   TV lower bound (Cor. 1a) makes the *upper-bound certificate* vacuous — yet it is
   the best operator. This confirms the bound is one-directional (a large TV does
   not imply large risk) and is NOT used as a preference argument; the
   density-matching complement (FCM-PM 0.654) does not beat it (Sec. 5
   density-shift refutation).
2. Pair-trained models extrapolate to higher-order mixes (P1 closure) —
   VERDICT: partial. Bit-level detection extrapolates (2/3/4-mix bitF1
   0.784/0.671/0.628); joint prediction does not (exact 0.699/0.143/0.000),
   and naive higher-order synthesis hurts. Refinement: real higher-order
   mixes are drawn from a SPARSE combination support (12/56 3-combos, 4/70
   4-combos exist), violating the independence assumption. The delta term of
   P1 can represent the oracle's knowledge of the combination support — one
   thing single-label data cannot supply. This sharpens, not breaks, the
   theory: closure holds at the evidence (bit) level where v is genuinely
   associative; it fails at the joint level where co-occurrence is
   structured.
3. Operator ranking follows measured fidelity (P2) — confirmed 3/3 families.
4. Outside blind-combination domains (natural RGB), conditional mismatch
   removes the zero-shift guarantee (Corollary 2). A positive risk gap is
   empirical, not implied by TV alone; VOC provides the measured boundary case.
5. Conformal tau bounds FAR at alpha given exchangeability (P3) — VERDICT:
   verified in the real-calibration regime (n=500 known-good samples:
   alpha=0.05 -> realized 0.040; alpha=0.01 -> 0.006; coverage >= 99.5%).
   Synthetic-normal calibration fails (realized FAR 0.97 at tau 0.045):
   training on those synthetic normals collapses their scores, so the
   score-space shift to real normals is large — exchangeability is the
   binding assumption. Practically benign: known-good normals require no
   defect-type or location annotation, although known-good status is still a
   label that must be established.
6. The label-fidelity criterion selects the operator per domain because the
   evidence geometry depends on D_real — VERDICT: confirmed across regimes.
   Superposition-structured domains (wafer, inked digits, audio) all take
   summation/union: on WM38 max-union (= Shin 2022) wins bit-F1 0.80, and on
   FSD50K waveform-sum wins 0.433 vs 0.27-0.31; disjoint-coordinate text takes
   averaging (vec-average > concat). Wafer and audio AGREE (both summation), so
   there is no cross-regime flip between them — the only flip is to averaging on
   text. Density mismatch (Cor. 1a) is retained only as a general TV lower-bound
   illustration, not a preference or penalty argument.
