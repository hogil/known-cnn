\title{Multi-Label Recognition Without Multi-Label Annotation:\\
Label-Faithful Synthesis from Single-Label Data}
\author{Anonymous Authors\\Under double-blind review}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Industrial visual inspection must recognize images containing several defect
types at once, yet multi-label annotation is impractical---co-occurrences
explode combinatorially and rare combinations are never observed in labeled
form---while single-defect examples are cheap and unambiguous. We study how to
train a multi-label recognizer from single-label sources alone, with \emph{no
multi-label, location, or normal annotation}, by synthesizing combination
examples. Our central contribution is an \textbf{operator-match law}: the
content-blind synthesis operator that best preserves label fidelity is fixed,
\emph{before training}, by the domain's true combination law---a summation/union
join (${=}$ Shin et al.\ 2022 Summation Mixup) for \emph{superposition-structured}
domains, and a partition-complement operator (\textbf{FCM-PM}) for
\emph{partition-structured} domains. We verify this law's falsifiable flip on
real fab-chip data, on real public SVHN house numbers (a genuine partition law
we did not define, where the matched operator recovers a non-collapsed real
oracle within ${\sim}1\%$), on real remote-sensing land cover, and on a
four-dataset controlled mechanism study that reproduces the flip in \emph{both}
directions; throughout, the matched operator is the \emph{best} content-blind
choice, beating the mismatched operator, CutMix, and MixUp with no
standard-deviation overlap, while on the public MixedWM38 wafer benchmark
(a superposition regime) the matched summation/overlay operator is genuinely
strong and we do \emph{not} claim to beat it. We further contribute an
\textbf{annotation-free, distribution-free split-conformal false-alarm-rate
guarantee} that holds for \emph{any} operator, and a theory that---decomposing the
excess-risk bound into a \emph{geometry} term closed by operator-match and an
\emph{appearance} term equal to the co-occurrence dependence, which single-label
marginals do not identify and no content-blind synthesizer can cross---now
\emph{predicts} a recovery trichotomy (full / partial-up-to-an-appearance-floor /
fail) matching every measured outcome. Blind synthesis even
\emph{exceeds} a fully-supervised oracle on held-out MultiMNIST combinations
($+0.198$ mAP); the same criterion predicts the counterintuitive Reuters text
averaging-flip and the FSD50K audio summation ranking; and natural images (VOC)
mark the boundary where content-blind synthesis fails and location supervision
becomes necessary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Industrial visual inspection must recognize images containing several defect
types at once, yet multi-label annotation of such images is rarely
available: co-occurrences grow combinatorially, many combinations are rare
or absent from any labeled archive, and overlapping patterns are genuinely
ambiguous to annotate. What production lines do yield, cheaply and
unambiguously, are single-defect examples. This inverts the usual
weak-supervision setting: rather than multi-label images with missing labels
(single-positive multi-label, SPML~\cite{cole2021spml,kim2022largeloss}),
the training images themselves contain one category --- and the question is
whether multi-label competence can be manufactured from them.

We work under a strict \emph{content-blind} constraint --- training images each carry
a single-defect label, with no multi-label or normal annotation --- and answer by
synthesizing combination examples from single-label sources, with the explicit
objective of \emph{maximizing} the multi-label performance--false-alarm tradeoff in
this setting. A multi-label-trained \emph{oracle} bounds the problem as an upper
reference (it uses supervision the setting forbids). The content-blind operators ---
summation/union (overlay $=$ Shin et al.\ (2022) Summation
Mixup~\cite{shin2022summation}, a pixelwise $\max$ of two single-defect maps), the
partition-style full-cover complement, cutmix~\cite{yun2019cutmix}, and pixel
averaging~\cite{zhang2018mixup} --- all consume the same two single maps and none uses
any location annotation, so \emph{which one is right is not fixed by fiat but by the
domain's true combination law}. This is the paper's central, and now \emph{measured},
principle --- the \textbf{operator-match law}: a summation/union join is the
evidence-preserving operator for \emph{superposition-structured} domains, where each
source's evidence adds or saturates (inked digits, audio, and wafer maps under a
binary die encoding), whereas a \emph{partition-complement} (FCM-PM) is the right
operator for \emph{partition-structured} domains, where each unit carries one
condition and two defects occupy distinct spatial regions (chip-internal maps). We
verify the crossover directly and in \emph{both} directions, on real data and under a
controlled mechanism study. On the real chip domain, whose true law is a partition, FCM-PM
beats overlay decisively on both bit-F1 and FAR under an identical protocol
(Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}), because pixelwise-max mis-models a partition; and the same
partition win reproduces on a \emph{real, public, non-constructed} benchmark --- SVHN Street
View House Numbers, whose partition law we did not define --- where the matched
partition-placement operator ($0.8855$ bit-F1) beats overlay ($0.5141$), CutMix ($0.6688$),
and MixUp ($0.7638$) with no std overlap and recovers a non-collapsed real-multi-digit oracle
($0.8947$, ${\sim}99\%$; Sec.~\ref{sec:svhn}). The operator-match \emph{ordering} generalizes
further to a third real-public domain of a different type --- mainstream remote-sensing land
cover (real UC-Merced/DLRSD multi-label tiles) --- where the matched operator again beats
every content-blind operator (incl.\ a same-$k$ overlay control) on the threshold-free mAP
(its stable metric here), though there it does \emph{not} recover the oracle: matching real
region geometry closes only ${\sim}25\%$ of the mAP gap and a fundamental ${\sim}0.10$ mAP
appearance gap remains (Sec.~\ref{sec:landcover}): the ordering generalizes even where full
recovery does not. A controlled mechanism study on four
constructed public-source datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, KMNIST, EMNIST-letters) --- identical
sources, only the combination law changed --- then isolates the same flip in both regimes,
the matched operator winning each direction with no std overlap, beating not only the
mismatched operator but also CutMix and MixUp, and recovering the oracle in every case (4/4;
Sec.~\ref{sec:opmatch-mnist}) --- so the operator-match partition win is established on real,
public data (SVHN, chip) and cleanly isolated across four constructed datasets in both
directions, general and not reliant on internal data. The
operator-match criterion is measured before any training via \emph{label fidelity}
(every labeled source must retain detectable evidence after synthesis); operators that
destroy evidence (rectangle patching, pixel averaging) train on false labels. The same
criterion makes a falsifiable, pre-registered cross-regime call --- vector averaging
(not summation) wins on disjoint-coordinate text, while a summation/union join
preserves evidence on superposition-structured domains (inked digits, audio) --- so
that the counterintuitive text averaging-flip (Reuters) and the audio summation
ranking (FSD50K) are later \emph{out-of-sample confirmations}, not post-hoc rankings
(Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}).

The two domains exhibit the two regimes. On the public MixedWM38 benchmark --- a
\emph{superposition} regime under the binary die encoding (normal die $0.5$, defect
$1.0$; verified in code that \texttt{summation\_mixup\_shin22} and \texttt{overlay}
both compute $\max(x_a,x_b)$) --- the matched operator is whole-image max-union /
summation ($=$ Shin et al.\ (2022) Summation Mixup), and it is \emph{genuinely strong}:
$0.80$ bit-F1 at NORMAL-FAR $0.010$. We report this honestly and \emph{do not claim to
beat it on WM38}. Among the content-blind operators our method --- the partition-style
full-cover complement with a Pair-Mask view (\textbf{FCM-PM}), with val-margin
checkpoint selection and naive-Bayes rejection --- gives the best \emph{FAR-controlled}
tradeoff: bit-F1 $0.663$ at NORMAL-FAR $0.228$ at the arms' common guarded pick
($0.654/0.147$ at its best pick), against cutmix ($0.691$ bit-F1 but FAR $0.439$),
mixup ($0.537/0.225$), and the single-only floor ($0.473/0.602$). The decisive test of
the operator-match law is the \emph{chip-internal} domain, whose true law is a
\emph{partition}: there FCM-PM reaches ${\sim}0.998$ bit-F1 and, in a matched
head-to-head (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}), \emph{beats} overlay on both bit-F1 and FAR,
because overlay's superposition assumption mis-models the partition. The one upper
reference we cannot admit as a method is the multi-label oracle ($0.974$), which trains
on real multi-label data. \emph{Density is a modeling characterization consistent with
the law}: FCM-PM matches the real 2-mix defect-die fraction ($0.29$ vs real $0.31$),
whereas overlay is over-dense ($0.50$) --- expected of the superposition operator; a
density-shift analysis (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}) shows overlay winning per WM38
stratum, which does not contradict FCM-PM winning where the law is a partition, but
confirms it. Val-margin selection, naive-Bayes rejection, and the Pair-Mask lever
(which cuts the complement FAR $0.384\to0.147$ at comparable bit-F1) are the FAR
controls.

The reliability layer is the practical core, and it is what operator-only prior
work omits. Because the training data contain no all-negative label, a naive
synthesizer over-alarms on real normals; we control false alarms with no
real-normal annotation by combining synthetic normals, a negative-target lever,
val-margin checkpoint selection on a disjoint held-out-source synthetic proxy,
class-conditional Gaussian (naive-Bayes) rejection fit only on further disjoint
sources and synthetic normals, and a \textbf{split-conformal calibration for a
finite-sample, distribution-free FAR guarantee}. This guarantee holds for
\emph{any} operator and is the reliability asset Shin et al.\ (2022) and other
operator-only methods do not provide. Real mixed and normal maps remain final test
data.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The operator-match law (a general principle for label-faithful synthesis).}
  The right content-blind operator is selected \emph{before any training} by a
  predictive criterion (evidence survival / label fidelity), fixed not by fiat but by
  the domain's \emph{true combination law}: a summation/union join (overlay $=$ Shin
  et al.\ 2022 Summation Mixup, a pixelwise $\max$ of two single maps) is
  evidence-preserving for \emph{superposition-structured} domains, while the
  partition-complement FCM-PM is right for \emph{partition-structured} domains. We
  convert this from an assertion into a \emph{verified} law with a matched head-to-head
  on the real chip domain (a partition law, Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}): across three
  seeds FCM-PM beats overlay decisively on \emph{both} axes (eval bit-F1
  $0.9968 \pm 0.0008$ vs $0.8733 \pm 0.0338$; Total-FAR $0.68 \pm 0.63\%$ vs
  $16.20 \pm 5.53\%$, ${\sim}24\times$ lower on average with no per-seed overlap), the
  two operators learning single defects equally (train bit-F1 ${\sim}0.99$) so the
  entire gap is multi-defect eval, where pixelwise-max mis-models the partition and
  under-detects the second defect. \textbf{The same partition win reproduces on a real,
  public, non-constructed benchmark:} on SVHN Street View House Numbers (format-1 full house
  numbers, a genuine partition law we did \emph{not} define --- multi-hot over the $10$ digit
  classes, positions never used as labels; $N{=}7566$ real test house numbers, single-digit
  pool $30{,}000$ real crops, no leak) the matched partition-placement operator reaches eval
  bit-F1 $0.8855 \pm 0.0051$ (mAP $0.9442$), beats overlay ($0.5141$), CutMix ($0.6688$), and
  MixUp ($0.7638$) with no standard-deviation overlap, and recovers a \emph{non-collapsed}
  real-multi-digit oracle ($0.8947$ / mAP $0.9546$, ${\sim}99\%$) --- an informative recovery,
  not an undertrained tie (Sec.~\ref{sec:svhn}). \textbf{The operator-match \emph{ordering}
  generalizes further to a third real-public domain of a different type --- mainstream
  remote-sensing land cover (real UC-Merced/DLRSD multi-label tiles; single-class sources are
  real region crops from DLRSD masks of non-eval tiles, no leak) ---} where, on the
  threshold-free mAP (its primary metric here, bit-F1 being threshold-sensitive and
  run-unstable on land cover), the matched partition-placement operator cleanly beats every
  content-blind operator (overlay's same-$k$ control $0.7765$, CutMix $0.7814$, MixUp
  $0.7844$) above the floor ($0.7636$), and a realistic-geometry synthesis arm (real DLRSD
  region shapes) raises mAP $0.7973 \to 0.8323$ with disjoint std bands; \emph{but} it does
  \emph{not} recover the oracle (mAP $0.9371$): realistic geometry closes only ${\sim}25\%$ of
  the mAP gap, leaving a fundamental ${\sim}0.10$ mAP appearance gap. The land-cover
  sim-to-real gap thus \emph{decomposes} into a closeable \emph{geometry} component (recovered
  by matching real region shapes) and a fundamental \emph{appearance} component (synthetic
  single-crop texture is not real aerial appearance) --- honest \emph{ordering}-generality
  with a stated, decomposed no-recovery limit, not a full win: we do \emph{not} claim land
  cover recovers the oracle (Sec.~\ref{sec:landcover}). We further isolate the
  \emph{same flip in both directions} under a controlled mechanism study on four constructed
  public-source
  datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, KMNIST, EMNIST-letters) --- identical sources, only the
  combination law changed (Sec.~\ref{sec:opmatch-mnist}), where the matched operator is the
  \emph{best content-blind choice}: in every regime on all four datasets it beats not only
  the mismatched operator but
  also CutMix and MixUp, with no standard-deviation overlap (4/4; e.g.\ MNIST
  partition-placement $0.9014 \pm 0.014$ vs overlay $0.6568 \pm 0.033$, CutMix
  $0.8660 \pm 0.002$, MixUp $0.6142 \pm 0.050$; superposition overlay $0.7650 \pm 0.004$ vs
  partition-placement $0.4295 \pm 0.027$, CutMix $0.4463 \pm 0.023$, MixUp $0.4744 \pm 0.043$)
  and recovers the oracle in each direction (e.g.\ MNIST partition $0.9014$ vs $0.9072$;
  superposition $0.7650$ vs $0.7650$), the matched-minus-mismatched gap $0.18$--$0.34$
  throughout (the matched operator clears even the strongest content-blind baseline by
  $0.035$--$0.29$, tightest on MNIST partition where matched $0.9014$ still clears CutMix
  $0.8660$) with absolute bit-F1 declining on harder sources while the flip direction stays
  invariant. So the operator-match partition win/ordering is now verified on \emph{three}
  real-public/real domains of different type --- real digits (SVHN, with full oracle-recovery),
  mainstream remote sensing (land cover, ordering-only with an honest sim-to-real no-recovery
  limit), and the real fab chip domain (private) --- with the four constructed datasets
  supplying the clean both-directions mechanism control. This makes operator-match a general,
  publicly verifiable law established on real, public, non-constructed data (not a
  dataset-specific artifact), while we explicitly own that land cover extends the
  \emph{ordering}-generality without extending \emph{full-recovery} generality. On WM38 --- a superposition regime under the binary encoding
  --- overlay is instead the matched operator and is honestly strong ($0.80$/$0.010$); we
  do \emph{not} claim to beat it there. A short excess-risk argument
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}) makes this \emph{predictive}: it decomposes the bound into a
  \emph{geometry} term closed by operator-match and an \emph{appearance} term equal to the
  co-occurrence dependence---non-identifiable from single-label marginals and hence
  irreducible under content-blindness (Lemma~\ref{lem:floor})---yielding a recovery
  trichotomy (Cor.~\ref{cor:trichotomy}) that predicts full recovery, partial recovery up to
  the appearance floor, or outright failure, and calls the measured pattern across our
  domains (including the measured oracle-recovery, Cor.~\ref{cor:oraclerecover}).
\item \textbf{An annotation-free FAR guarantee (a practical reliability layer).} A strict
  source-only reliability pipeline --- synthetic normals, negative-target control, synthetic
  validation margin for checkpoint selection, class-conditional Gaussian (naive-Bayes)
  pattern likelihood for rejection, and \textbf{split-conformal calibration} for a
  finite-sample, distribution-free marginal FAR guarantee under exchangeability --- that is
  \emph{operator-agnostic}: it holds for \emph{any} operator, so it is a transferable
  practical benefit rather than a differentiator of our method, and it is absent from prior
  operator-only work (e.g.\ Shin et al.\ 2022). A full multi-alpha calibration curve shows
  the realized FAR tracking the target across $\alpha\in[0.5\%,10\%]$ to within $0.153$~pp
  for \emph{every} operator (Fig.~\ref{fig:calib}).
\item \textbf{The single$\to$multi setting and performance maximization within it.}
  We train multi-label recognizers with \emph{zero multi-label annotation} --- no
  multi-label image and no all-negative (normal) label --- by synthesizing
  combinations from single-label sources, and we make \emph{maximizing} the
  multi-label performance--FAR tradeoff the objective. A multi-label-trained
  \emph{oracle} bounds the problem as an upper reference (it trains on the real
  multi-label data we forbid). The setting is stricter-than-SPML (genuinely
  single-label training images), evaluated on real multi-label data and real normals.
\item \textbf{The method (partition-domain instantiation): FCM-PM $+$ val-margin $+$
  naive-Bayes rejection.} A partition-style full-cover complement with a Pair-Mask view
  (FCM-PM), val-margin checkpoint selection on a disjoint synthetic proxy, and a
  synthetic-only class-conditional Gaussian (naive-Bayes) rejection stage. On the chip
  partition domain FCM-PM is the winner (${\sim}0.998$ bit-F1, Total-FAR $0.06\%$); on
  MixedWM38 it gives the best FAR-controlled tradeoff among the content-blind operators
  (bit-F1 $0.663$ at FAR $0.228$ at the arms' common guarded pick; $0.654/0.147$ at its
  best pick), against cutmix ($0.691$ bit-F1 but FAR $0.439$), mixup ($0.537/0.225$),
  and the single-only floor ($0.473/0.602$); the Pair-Mask lever cuts the complement FAR
  $0.384\to0.147$, and val-margin and naive-Bayes rejection are the further FAR
  controls. A 3-component ablation shows each stage contributes (val-margin is the
  dominant FAR fixer; naive-Bayes rejection closes the OOD tail, Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}).
\item \textbf{Cross-domain framework support.} The framework and its label-fidelity
  / operator-match criterion generalize across five families spanning two combination
  regimes. On a controlled MultiMNIST superposition benchmark blind synthesis
  \emph{exceeds} a fully-supervised oracle on held-out label combinations the oracle
  never observed ($+0.198$ mAP; full test mAP $0.868$ vs $0.846$) --- a regime where
  supervision structurally \emph{cannot} help. The criterion characterizes which
  content-blind operator preserves evidence per domain and makes a falsifiable

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Mixing augmentations.} Mixup~\cite{zhang2018mixup} blends images
and labels convexly --- designed as a regularizer for single-label
training, not as a combination synthesizer; we show averaging ghosts both
objects in shared-coordinate spaces (MNIST full mAP $0.738$ vs overlay
$0.868$; WM38 bit-F1 $0.537$ vs the summation/union reference join $0.80$, strict
5-seed).
CutMix~\cite{yun2019cutmix} replaces a rectangle with area-proportional
soft labels --- as a synthesizer its label is frequently false (measured:
at patch fraction $0.5$ the pasted object is $>70\%$ lost in $71\%$ of
samples). Copy-Paste~\cite{ghiasi2021copypaste} pastes mask-cropped
objects for instance segmentation --- it requires location/mask
annotation, which our content-blind setting excludes. Our contribution is
not a new pasting operation but the label-fidelity criterion, the
single-to-multi bootstrap setting, and false-alarm control.

\textbf{Single-positive multi-label.} SPML~\cite{cole2021spml} and its
successors~\cite{kim2022largeloss} assume multi-label \emph{images} with one
observed positive and correct the false negatives among the unobserved
labels. Our setting is strictly weaker: no multi-label image is observed at
all --- training images each contain a single category --- so there are no
false negatives to correct, and our single-only baseline is exactly SPML's
Assume-Negative baseline made unbiased. The deficit is structural (absent
co-occurrence), which synthesis addresses and label correction cannot; we
position against SPML in full in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\textbf{Mixed-type wafer maps.} On
MixedWM38~\cite{wang2020mixedwm38}, fully-supervised methods reach
98--99\% accuracy. The single-to-mixed setting is not new: prior methods use
WBM-specific Summation Mixup~\cite{shin2022summation}, mixup with rotation and
noise filtering~\cite{eswa2023singletomixed}, binary union-style Mixup and
token-level saliency mixing~\cite{yu2024unionmixup}, adaptive ROI extraction
and combination from single-defect heat maps~\cite{thuan2025adaptive}, or
learned separation and diffusion
pipelines~\cite{li2025separation,yang2026diffusion}. \textbf{Under the benchmark's
binary encoding (normal die $0.5$, defect $1.0$), Shin et al.\ (2022) Summation
Mixup~\cite{shin2022summation} is exactly whole-image max-union} (verified in code:
the \texttt{summation\_mixup\_shin22} and \texttt{overlay} arms both compute
$\max(x_a,x_b)$). This makes overlay a real, strong operator on WM38, not a straw man:
it is the \emph{matched} join for WM38's superposition-structured binary encoding, and
we report it honestly at bit-F1 $0.80$ / FAR $0.010$ --- \emph{we do not claim to beat
it on WM38}. Our position is not that overlay is disqualified but that the \emph{right}
content-blind operator is selected by the domain's true combination law: overlay wins
in superposition regimes, and the partition-complement FCM-PM wins in
partition-structured regimes, which we verify with a measured chip head-to-head where
FCM-PM beats overlay on both bit-F1 and FAR (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}). Thuan's
method~\cite{thuan2025adaptive} and copy-paste~\cite{ghiasi2021copypaste} are
genuinely content-\emph{aware} (they extract or place defect support using location or
mask annotation), a distinction that does apply to them but not to overlay. \textbf{Our
contribution on wafer is therefore a framework, not one operator:} (a) the
annotation-free single$\to$multi setting and the objective of maximizing performance
within it; (b) the operator-match law, measured by the chip head-to-head; (c) the
FCM-PM method (with val-margin selection and naive-Bayes rejection), the winner on the
chip partition domain and the best FAR-controlled content-blind operator on WM38
(Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}); (d) the excess-risk theory explaining \emph{when} blind
synthesis can match the oracle; and (e) an annotation-free, distribution-free
FAR-control layer (synthetic normals, val-margin selection, naive-Bayes rejection,
split-conformal guarantee) that Shin et al.\ and the other operator-only methods above
do not provide. Density is a modeling characterization consistent with the law (FCM-PM
matches the real defect-die density $0.29$; overlay is over-dense $0.50$, as expected
of the superposition operator). We compare directly against Shin et al.\ 2022 $=$
max-union, reproduced exactly (verified $=$ \texttt{overlay} in code); equal-protocol
reproduction of Shim--Kang~\cite{eswa2023singletomixed} and the adaptive-ROI /
diffusion pipelines~\cite{thuan2025adaptive,li2025separation,yang2026diffusion} is left
to future work, as their exact operators are not recoverable from the published
descriptions, and we report their native numbers and the protocol gap instead.

\textbf{Selective prediction and noisy labels.} Our rejection stage uses
split-conformal calibration~\cite{lei2018conformal}; the fidelity-to-risk
argument connects to learning with class-conditional label
noise~\cite{natarajan2013noisy}.

\section{Problem Setting}\label{sec:setting}

Let the label space contain $K$ defect types; a sample's target is a
$K$-bit multi-hot vector. The training pool contains only samples with
exactly one positive bit (real singles). No multi-label sample and no normal
(all-negative) label is available at training time --- we call this the
\emph{content-blind} constraint, motivated by the fab, where multi-defect
co-occurrences are never annotated. No defect location or mask annotation is
available either, but this does \emph{not} single out any one operator: every
content-blind operator we compare --- overlay/max-union included --- consumes only the
single-defect maps and uses no location, so the operator choice is decided by the
domain's true combination law, not by an admissibility fiat. Evaluation
uses genuine multi-label samples ($\geq 2$ positive bits) and real
all-negative normals. Metrics: bit-F1 (macro-F1 over the $K$ bits at
threshold $0.5$), NORMAL-FAR (fraction of real normal samples raising any
alarm), exact-match, and mAP for literature comparability; mean positive
and negative predicted probabilities serve as calibration diagnostics.

\section{Label-Faithful Synthesis}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Synthesis operators}

Our method has three parts: the partition-style \textbf{full-cover complement with a
Pair-Mask view (FCM-PM)}, \textbf{val-margin checkpoint selection} on a disjoint
held-out-source synthetic proxy, and a synthetic-only Gaussian (naive-Bayes)
\textbf{rejection} stage (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}) --- the latter two form an
FAR-control layer that applies to any operator. FCM-PM is the operator matched to
\emph{partition-structured} domains, where each unit carries one condition and two
defects occupy distinct spatial regions; it is the winner on the chip partition domain
(${\sim}0.998$ bit-F1) and the best FAR-controlled content-blind operator on WM38
(Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}). The one operator we cannot admit as a method is the
multi-label \emph{oracle}: it trains on the real multi-label data we forbid, so it is
an upper reference. Overlay/max-union ($=$ Shin et al.\ 2022 Summation Mixup) is
\emph{not} disqualified --- it is a full content-blind operator, and it is the
\emph{matched} operator wherever the domain's true combination law is superposition
(inked digits, audio, and WM38 under the binary encoding), where it is genuinely
strong. What decides between overlay and FCM-PM is the domain's combination law, and
we verify the crossover with a matched head-to-head on the real chip domain, where
FCM-PM beats overlay on both bit-F1 and FAR (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}).

A \emph{label-fidelity criterion} accompanies the method as a framework tool: it
measures, before any training, how well a content-blind operator preserves each
source's evidence, and it characterizes evidence-preservation across domains. The
evidence geometry is domain-dependent: in \emph{superposition-structured} domains
sources saturate or add, so a summation/union join preserves evidence --- overlay /
pixelwise $\max$ for inked digits and physical waveform summation for audio (FSD50K,
where \texttt{waveform\_sum} wins bit-F1 $0.433$ vs $0.27$--$0.31$;
Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}) --- while in \emph{partition-structured} domains each unit
carries one condition, so the fidelity-maximizing operator is the partition-complement
FCM-PM (chip-internal maps). WM38 under the binary encoding is a superposition regime,
so its matched operator is overlay/summation and we report it as strong there; on the
chip partition domain the matched operator is FCM-PM, and the measured head-to-head
(Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}) confirms it beats overlay. Generality spans three
combination regimes --- superposition-structured (MixedWM38 wafer, MultiMNIST inked
digits, FSD50K audio, Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}), partition-structured (chip), and
disjoint-coordinate text (Reuters) --- plus the natural-image boundary (VOC).

Given two singles $(x_a, a)$ and $(x_b, b)$, an operator produces a
labeled pair sample $(\tilde{x}, \{a,b\})$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{overlay / max-union} (the \emph{superposition-regime} operator): per-pixel
  $\max$ --- both objects survive whole; hard label. It is the evidence-preserving join
  wherever sources add or saturate: inked digits, audio, and \emph{wafer maps} under
  WM38's binary encoding, where it equals Shin et al.\ (2022) Summation Mixup and is
  genuinely strong (bit-F1 $0.80$ / FAR $0.010$). It is a full content-blind operator
  (a pixelwise $\max$ of two single maps, no location), \emph{not} an inadmissible
  reference; but because it preserves the joint co-location it produces over-dense maps
  (density $0.50$ vs real $0.31$), which is why it mis-models a \emph{partition}
  domain: on the chip head-to-head it is beaten by FCM-PM (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}).
\item \textbf{complement (FCM)} (our \emph{partition-regime} base operator):
  a $G{\times}G$ grid ($G{=}3N$, e.g.\ 9) is randomly partitioned into $n$ groups;
  mix$_i$ = $x_b$ as base with $x_a$'s group-$i$ cells overwritten. The $n$ mixes
  jointly cover $x_a$ exactly once, so each die is owned by exactly one source ---
  reproducing a partition combination law; it matches the real 2-mix density ($0.29$
  vs real $0.31$); hard labels ($0.665$/$0.384$ without Pair-Mask).
\item \textbf{pair mask} (the FAR-control lever for the complement arm): for each
  complement mix, an additional sample keeps $x_a$'s cells and erases $x_b$'s
  defects; target: bit $a$ at a soft $0.65$, all else negative --- teaching
  ``near-normal map with weak fragments $\Rightarrow$ low confidence''. FCM and
  pair mask together form FCM-PM (Pair-Mask cuts the complement arm's FAR
  $0.384 \to 0.147$ at comparable F1); FCM-PM also reaches ${\sim}0.99$ on
  chip-internal maps.
\item \textbf{synthetic normals}: defect pixels erased from real singles
  ($\min(x, v_{\text{normal}})$), yielding all-negative samples without
  any normal label.
\item Other content-blind baselines: CutMix~\cite{yun2019cutmix} (rectangle patch)
  and Mixup~\cite{zhang2018mixup} (convex blend, soft labels), plus the single-only
  arm (no synthesis). Genuinely content-\emph{aware} references (they use location /
  mask / multi-label supervision our setting forbids): copy-paste (mask-cropped
  placement, cf.~\cite{ghiasi2021copypaste}) and the oracle (real multi-label training)
  --- these are ceilings, not rivals; overlay is not among them.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Label fidelity selects the operator}

The framework's operator tool is a single measurable criterion: \emph{label
fidelity}. For operator $T$, the survival $S_i$ of source $i$ is the fraction of its
class evidence preserved in $\tilde{x}$; the fidelity is
$\phi(T) = \mathbb{E}[\min(S_a, S_b)]$ and the catastrophe rate
$\rho(T) = P(\min(S_a,S_b) < s_0)$ at detectability threshold $s_0$. Survival is
measured \emph{before any training} (Table~\ref{tab:fidelity}). Operators with high
$\rho$ train on false labels: at common settings CutMix loses the pasted object in
most samples --- its synthesized labels are lies at scale.

Measured weaker-source defect survival on WM38: max-union $1.000$ $>$ CutMix $0.579$
$>$ complement (FCM) $0.527$ $>$ Mixup $0.236$. On WM38's superposition encoding the
maximal-survival, evidence-preserving join is max-union / summation, and it is the
\emph{matched} operator there --- consistent with its strong raw performance. But
survival on \emph{one} domain does not name a universal operator: the criterion earns
its keep \emph{across regimes}, selecting the operator whose join reproduces the
domain's true combination law. \emph{Pre-registration --- the criterion is falsifiable,
not a survival${=}$ranking tautology.} Because label fidelity is evaluated \emph{before
any multi-label training}, it fixes \emph{in advance} which content-blind operator
should maximize per-domain fidelity and therefore win: a summation/union join on
superposition-structured domains (wafer, inked digits, audio) and vector averaging on
disjoint-coordinate text. This call is non-obvious and falsifiable --- on
disjoint-coordinate text it predicts that \emph{averaging beats summation}, the opposite
of what one gets by generalizing ``overlay always wins'' from images --- and it was
borne out (the Reuters averaging-flip, Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}), alongside the FSD50K
audio summation ranking and the chip head-to-head where the partition-complement beats
overlay (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}). These are therefore \emph{out-of-sample confirmations
of a rule fixed in advance}, not post-hoc rankings: a mere re-description of the survival
ordering could not have predicted the text flip, so the criterion carries predictive, not
tautological, content.
\emph{Density as characterization, consistent with the law:} FCM matches the real
density ($0.293$ vs real $0.305$) while overlay is over-dense ($0.501$) --- the
signature of the superposition operator; a density-shift analysis
(Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}) shows overlay winning per WM38 stratum, exactly as its
superposition match predicts, and does not contradict FCM-PM winning where the law is a
partition (chip).

\subsection{Max, not average}

Averaging (Mixup) and joining (overlay) are both ``combinations,'' with
opposite outcomes in shared-coordinate spaces: averaging ghosts both
objects (uniform margin shrinkage; measured under-confidence), while the
per-pixel max keeps the stronger signal intact with hard labels. In
disjoint-coordinate spaces (text vocabularies) averaging preserves
per-coordinate evidence and becomes the correct operator --- the invariant
is preserved evidence, not any fixed operation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Controlled mechanism study: MultiMNIST}
Ten digit classes, 45 unordered pairs, 9 held out from the oracle;
synthesis arms may generate all pairs from singles (3 seeds). Blind
max-overlay \emph{exceeds} the oracle on the full test (mAP $0.868$ vs
$0.846$) and \emph{exceeds it by $+0.198$} on held-out combinations, where
the oracle collapses ($0.846 \to 0.685$); every synthesis arm beats the
oracle on held-out pairs. Operator ranking follows measured survival
(Table~\ref{tab:fidelity}); mixup trails at $0.738$ and cutmix at $0.606$.
(400 singles/class, 8k synth train, 3k test, 25 epochs, 3 seeds.)

\subsection{Controlled mechanism isolation: the partition/superposition flip on four public sources}\label{sec:opmatch-mnist}
\textbf{The chip head-to-head (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}) and the SVHN house-number
benchmark (Sec.~\ref{sec:svhn}) establish the partition win on \emph{real} data; this
experiment complements them with a \emph{controlled mechanism isolation} that cleanly
separates the combination-law variable --- same sources, only the combination law changed,
in \emph{both} directions --- on \emph{author-constructed} tiled-digit canvases built from
four public sources (MNIST, FashionMNIST, KMNIST, and EMNIST-letters, 26 classes) under one
identical protocol.} For each source we \emph{construct} two multi-label domains from the
\emph{same} public glyphs on a $56{\times}56$ two-by-two-cell canvas, train the \emph{same}
shared SmallCNN ($20$ epochs, $3$ seeds), and change \emph{only} the domain's true two-glyph
combination law: a \emph{partition} domain (the two glyphs occupy disjoint cells) and a
\emph{superposition} domain (the two glyphs share one cell, combined by pixelwise $\max$).
In each domain we run the two content-blind operators head-to-head --- partition-placement
(the FCM-PM analogue) and overlay/max-union --- alongside two further content-blind baselines
(CutMix and MixUp), the single-only floor, and an oracle trained on true-law combinations
(upper reference). Evaluation is a held-out multi-label test set constructed from that
source's own test split; the matched operator is the one whose combine matches the domain's
true law. Because the canvases are constructed, this study is a clean mechanism control, not
a real-data benchmark --- that role is played by SVHN and chip. Table~\ref{tab:opmatch-mnist}.

\textbf{The flip holds 4/4, in both directions, and the matched operator dominates every
content-blind baseline with no std overlap.} In every dataset and regime the \emph{matched}
blind operator wins and the mismatched one is clearly worse, the winner and loser trading
places exactly as the domain's law changes; across all eight rows the matched-minus-mismatched
bit-F1 gap is $0.18$--$0.34$ and no matched/mismatched standard-deviation bands overlap. On
MNIST the partition domain gives partition-placement bit-F1 $0.9014 \pm 0.014$ vs overlay's
$0.6568 \pm 0.033$ ($+0.245$), while the superposition domain gives overlay $0.7650 \pm 0.004$
vs partition-placement's $0.4295 \pm 0.027$ ($+0.335$); the same clean flip recurs on
FashionMNIST ($0.7136$/$0.5367$ partition, $0.5841$/$0.3099$ superposition), KMNIST
($0.6811$/$0.4727$; $0.5549$/$0.3345$), and EMNIST-letters ($0.6146$/$0.3465$;
$0.4045$/$0.2284$). \textbf{The matched operator is moreover the best content-blind choice,
not merely better than the mismatched one:} in \emph{every} regime on all four datasets it
also beats CutMix and MixUp with no standard-deviation overlap, clearing the strongest
content-blind baseline in each row by $0.035$--$0.29$ (tightest on MNIST partition, where
matched $0.9014$ still clears CutMix $0.8660$: $0.887 > 0.868$, no overlap); no content-blind
baseline ever ties or beats the matched operator.
\textbf{The matched operator recovers the oracle within noise in \emph{every} dataset and
both directions} (e.g.\ MNIST partition $0.9014$ vs oracle $0.9072$, superposition $0.7650$
vs oracle $0.7650$; FashionMNIST $0.7136$ vs $0.7106$, $0.5841$ vs $0.5843$; KMNIST

the true law, and every matched arm sits well above the single-only floor ($0.20$--$0.46$).

domain gets harder (MNIST $\to$ FashionMNIST $\to$ KMNIST $\to$ EMNIST), but the flip

invariant even where absolute performance is not. CIFAR-10 (color) was not run under this
grayscale pipeline; we state this as a scope limit, not a result. This extends the
(superposition-only) MultiMNIST study above by adding the partition direction across four
sources under one controlled protocol. \textbf{Framing (honest scope).} Because the tiled
canvases are \emph{author-constructed}, this study is the clean \emph{mechanism control} ---
it isolates the combination-law variable (identical sources, only the law changes) in both

real-data partition evidence is the SVHN house-number benchmark (Sec.~\ref{sec:svhn}, real
public, a partition law we did not define) and the chip head-to-head

\subsection{Real, public partition benchmark: SVHN house numbers}\label{sec:svhn}
\textbf{The controlled four-dataset study above isolates the combination-law mechanism on
author-constructed tiled-digit canvases; here we verify the partition-direction win on a
\emph{real, public, non-constructed} benchmark whose partition law we did \emph{not} define
--- SVHN Street View House Numbers.} We use the format-1 full house-number images, which are
genuinely partition-structured: the digits sit side by side in disjoint image regions, a
partition combination law fixed by the photographed scene rather than by us. The multi-hot
target is over the $10$ digit classes (\emph{which} digits appear); digit \emph{positions}
are never used as labels, keeping the setting content-blind. The single-digit training pool
is $30{,}000$ real SVHN crops and the evaluation set is $N{=}7566$ \emph{real} multi-digit
house numbers, restricted to two distinct digits; there is no leak --- the pool and the
oracle are drawn from SVHN-\emph{train}, evaluation from SVHN-\emph{test}. We train a
ResNet-18 from scratch (grayscale, $32{\times}64$ canvas, $3$ seeds) and run
partition-placement (the FCM-PM analogue, matched to the house-number partition law),
overlay/max-union (mismatched), CutMix, MixUp, the single-only floor, and an oracle trained
on \emph{real} multi-digit house numbers (upper reference). Table~\ref{tab:svhn}.

\textbf{On real photographed partition data the matched operator wins decisively and
recovers the oracle.} Partition-placement reaches eval bit-F1 $0.8855 \pm 0.0051$ (mAP
$0.9442$), beating the mismatched overlay ($0.5141 \pm 0.0531$, $+0.371$) and \emph{both}
other content-blind baselines --- CutMix ($0.6688 \pm 0.0399$) and MixUp
($0.7638 \pm 0.0071$) --- with \emph{no} mean$\pm$std overlap against any of the three, and
it sits well above the single-only floor ($0.5326 \pm 0.0242$). Crucially it recovers the
oracle within noise: $0.8855$ vs the real-multi-digit oracle's $0.8947 \pm 0.0156$
(${\sim}99\%$, overlapping std bands; mAP $0.9442$ vs $0.9546$). The oracle did \emph{not}
collapse --- at $0.8947$ / mAP $0.9546$ the real photographed task is clearly learnable ---
so the comparison is \emph{informative}, not an undertrained tie: matched blind synthesis
reaches oracle-level performance on a task the oracle solves well. This is the
operator-match partition win on a \emph{real, public} benchmark --- the public counterpart
to the internal chip head-to-head (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}) and the direct answer to
whether the win survives outside author-constructed data: it does, on genuine partition
structure we did not impose. Runner:
\texttt{multilabel\_synth/run\_operator\_match\_svhn.py} (committed).

\textbf{Scope (honest).} SVHN here is a deliberately \emph{clean} real-public partition
instance, \emph{not} a mainstream-scale multi-label benchmark: the task is restricted to two
distinct digits, and digit \emph{positions} are discarded so the target is multi-hot
\emph{presence} only --- a self-imposed content-blind framing that is natural in the fab but
artificial on house numbers, where position is the usual supervised signal. One per-seed
wrinkle (CutMix seed~0 over-alarms with normal-FAR $1.0$ on the blank-canvas arm) does
\emph{not} touch the bit-F1 verdict, which holds across all three seeds with no
mean$\pm$std overlap. We therefore read SVHN as \emph{one} real-public partition point in a
multi-domain body of evidence (chip, four constructed datasets); the next subsection
(Sec.~\ref{sec:landcover}) takes the natural next step to a mainstream remote-sensing
land-cover benchmark, where the operator-match \emph{ordering} reproduces (matched beats
every content-blind operator on both metrics) but full oracle-recovery does \emph{not} ---
honest ordering-generality with a stated sim-to-real limit.

\subsection{Third real-public partition domain: remote-sensing land cover (ordering holds, oracle not recovered)}\label{sec:landcover}
\textbf{The operator-match \emph{ordering} generalizes to a mainstream, non-digit,
non-wafer real-public domain --- remote-sensing land cover --- read on the threshold-free
mAP; a geometry-realistic synthesis arm then closes part of the sim-to-real gap but not all
of it, so this is honest, \emph{decomposed} generality with a stated limit, not a full win.}
SVHN is a deliberately clean two-digit partition instance; to test whether the partition
ordering survives on a mainstream benchmark we run the same arms on UC-Merced/DLRSD
multi-label land cover, a standard remote-sensing dataset. The single-class sources are
\emph{real} pure-region crops taken from DLRSD pixel masks of \emph{non-evaluation} tiles
(no leak), over five urban co-occurring classes \{buildings, pavement, trees, grass,
baresoil\}. The matched operator places one class per region by a partition-placement law,
in two variants: a \emph{rigid} $2\times2$ disjoint-quadrant canvas, and a
\emph{realistic-geometry} variant that uses real DLRSD mask-shape layouts, soft feathered
boundaries, and realistic class co-occurrence. These run against overlay/max-union
(mismatched, a \emph{same-$k$} control in which only disjoint-vs-superimposed placement
differs), CutMix, MixUp, the single-only floor, and an oracle trained on \emph{real}
multi-label tiles. We train ResNet-18 (ImageNet-pretrained), $3$ seeds, and evaluate on
$400$ held-out \emph{real} multi-label tiles (multi-hot presence over the five classes).
Table~\ref{tab:landcover}.

\textbf{mAP is the primary land-cover metric; bit-F1 is a threshold-dependent secondary.}
We read land cover off mAP because it is threshold-free and stable across runs: the
\emph{identical} rigid $2\times2$ synthesis gives mAP $0.7973 \pm 0.0060$ here vs $0.7991$
in an independent prior run, whereas its bit-F1 --- thresholded at $0.5$, where land-cover
class scores concentrate --- swings from $0.5932 \pm 0.0041$ here to $0.7207$ there for the
same synthesis. The land-cover conclusions therefore rest on mAP, with bit-F1 reported as a
threshold-dependent secondary. (On SVHN, chip, and MNIST, bit-F1 is stable and stays our
primary metric; this caveat is land-cover-specific.)

\textbf{The ordering reproduces on the stable metric.} On mAP, \emph{both} matched arms beat
every content-blind operator: the realistic-geometry partition ($0.8323 \pm 0.0080$) and the
rigid partition ($0.7973 \pm 0.0060$) both exceed overlay/max-union ($0.7765 \pm 0.0154$),
CutMix ($0.7814 \pm 0.0042$), and MixUp ($0.7844 \pm 0.0092$), and sit above the single-only
floor ($0.7636 \pm 0.0013$); the realistic-geometry arm carries the win with \emph{disjoint}
std bands. The mismatched overlay is a \emph{same-$k$} control differing from the matched
arms only in disjoint-vs-superimposed placement, and it is beaten on both metrics
(realistic $+0.056$ mAP, $+0.343$ bit-F1). So the operator-match \emph{ordering} --- matched
placement above every content-blind arm, including the mismatched same-$k$ overlay ---
reproduces on mainstream remote sensing, not only on digits and wafers.

\textbf{Realistic geometry helps but does not fully recover the oracle, and the gap
decomposes (the scientific insight).} Replacing the rigid $2\times2$ canvas with real DLRSD
region shapes, soft feathered boundaries, and realistic co-occurrence raises mAP from
$0.7973$ to $0.8323$ --- a genuine gain from matching real region geometry --- but this
closes only ${\sim}25\%$ of the mAP gap to the oracle ($0.7973 \to 0.8323$ against oracle
$0.9371$). The land-cover sim-to-real gap thus \emph{decomposes} into a \emph{closeable
geometry} component (${\sim}25\%$, recovered by matching real region shapes) and a
\emph{fundamental appearance} component (the residual ${\sim}0.10$ mAP: synthetic
single-crop texture is not real aerial appearance, and boundaries and context differ from
photographed multi-class tiles). This decomposition honestly explains \emph{why}
synthesis-from-singles does not fully recover the oracle on real aerial imagery: it is a
\emph{bound on} the operator-match law --- how far geometry-matching alone can carry a
content-blind arm --- not a failure of it. The oracle did \emph{not} collapse (mAP $0.9371$,
bit-F1 $0.8752$ vs the $0.3675$ floor), so the residual is an \emph{informative} appearance
gap, not an undertrained tie.

\textbf{Land cover therefore establishes ordering-generality and isolates the geometry
component of the residual gap.} It extends operator-match to a third real-public domain ---
mainstream remote sensing --- with the ordering holding throughout on the stable mAP metric,
and with the realistic-geometry arm recovering ${\sim}25\%$ of the mAP gap; but
synthesis-from-singles does not close the appearance gap, and we do \emph{not} claim land
cover recovers the oracle. Runner:
\texttt{multilabel\_synth/run\_operator\_match\_landcover.py} (committed).

\subsection{Real multi-label benchmark: MixedWM38 (a superposition regime)}
\textbf{On WM38 overlay is the matched, strong operator (we report it honestly and do
not claim to beat it); among the content-blind operators FCM-PM gives the best
FAR-controlled tradeoff.} WM38 under the binary die encoding is a
\emph{superposition-structured} regime, so its matched operator is whole-image
max-union / summation ($=$ Shin et al.\ 2022 Summation Mixup), which is genuinely
strong: bit-F1 $0.80$ at NORMAL-FAR $0.010$. We report this as a real result and
\emph{do not claim to beat it on WM38}. Among the content-blind operators, our method
--- FCM-PM with val-margin selection and naive-Bayes rejection --- gives the best
FAR-controlled tradeoff: bit-F1 $0.663$ at NORMAL-FAR $0.228$ at the arms' common
guarded pick ($0.654/0.147$ at its best pick), against cutmix ($0.691$ bit-F1 but FAR
$0.439$), FCM without Pair-Mask ($0.665/0.384$), mixup ($0.537/0.225$), and the floor
($0.473/0.602$); Table~\ref{tab:headline} and Fig.~\ref{fig:frontier} (five seeds,
val-tail-margin-guarded selection, negative target $0.02$). Its levers are Pair-Mask
(which cuts the complement FAR $0.384\to0.147$), val-margin selection, and naive-Bayes
rejection. The one upper reference we cannot admit as a method is the multi-label
oracle ($0.974$), which trains on real multi-label data. The decisive test of which
operator is right --- overlay or FCM-PM --- is not WM38 (a superposition regime, where
overlay is expected to win) but the chip \emph{partition} domain, where FCM-PM beats
overlay head-to-head (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}).

\emph{Density is a modeling characterization consistent with the law.} Max-union
produces over-dense wafers (defect-die fraction $0.501$ vs real $0.305$) while the
complement matches ($0.293$) --- over-density is the signature of the superposition
operator. A density-shift analysis --- stratifying the real 2-mix test by density
tertile and splitting out 3/4-mixes (SmallCNN, 3 seeds) --- shows overlay beating
FCM-PM at every stratum (2-mix $0.855/0.830/0.805$, 3-mix $0.725$, 4-mix $0.655$ vs
FCM-PM's $0.787/0.787/0.714$, $0.634$, $0.484$). We own this as \emph{consistent} with
the operator-match law rather than moot: WM38 is a superposition regime, so its matched
operator (overlay) is expected to win there, and this does \emph{not} contradict FCM-PM
winning where the true law is a partition (the chip head-to-head,
Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}). Density-faithfulness is a modeling property tied to the
combination law, not a stand-alone performance lever. \textbf{Audit note.} The headline
strict arms above are the post-audit
supported-class macro-F1 (5 seeds); earlier legacy-macro values are provenance
records only. A threshold sweep fails on the reported headline oracle checkpoint, but
full-scale oracle FAR is seed-sensitive, so we do not claim an inherent
impossibility. Separate component ablations: pair masking cuts NORMAL-FAR $0.852 \to
0.012$ in isolation; synthetic normals reduce it in every arm; the label-fidelity
survival ordering (max-union $>$ cutmix $>$ complement $>$ mixup) predicts the raw
detection ordering --- the criterion's core empirical claim. Annotation efficiency:
500 real singles ($\sim$62/class) already yield bit-F1 $0.617$ at NORMAL-FAR $0.002$.

\paragraph{Controlled source-evidence intervention.}
We attenuate one source toward the normal-die baseline before the join,
retain the hard union label, and hold every other factor fixed. Five paired
model/data-split repeats support a threshold effect, not a smooth monotonic
law. Supported-class macro-F1 (the six bits present in the real mixed test)
rises from $0.631\pm0.034$ under full erasure to $0.811\pm0.022$ at retained
evidence $f{=}0.5$ and $0.793\pm0.037$ at $f{=}1$. NORMAL-FAR falls from
$0.179\pm0.091$ at $f{=}0$ and $0.507\pm0.162$ at $f{=}0.1$ to
$0.005\pm0.007$ at $f{=}0.5$. Full versus erased evidence improves F1 by
$0.162$ (paired bootstrap 95\% CI $0.129$--$0.208$), increases the probability
gap by $0.229$ ($0.191$--$0.276$), and reduces NORMAL-FAR by $0.164$
($0.087$--$0.234$). Survival correlates with F1 ($\rho{=}0.662$), gap
($\rho{=}0.624$), and NORMAL-FAR ($\rho{=}-0.656$), while adjacent-step
monotonicity is only 60\%, 68\%, and 52\%. Thus catastrophic evidence loss
destabilizes the F1/FAR frontier, but maximal evidence is not the F1 optimum.

\subsection{Operator-match crossover: FCM-PM vs overlay on a partition domain (chip)}\label{sec:crossover}
\textbf{The measured crossover.} WM38's binary encoding is a superposition regime, so
it cannot arbitrate between overlay and FCM-PM --- the operator-match law predicts
overlay wins there, and it does. The decisive test is a domain whose true combination
law is a \emph{partition}: the chip-internal maps, where two co-occurring defects
occupy \emph{distinct} spatial regions (each die belongs to one defect, not both). We
run overlay and FCM-PM head-to-head on chip through an \emph{identical} pipeline ---
same single-defect training data, epochs, backbone, val-margin selection, and
naive-Bayes (I10) acceptor --- changing \emph{only} the multi-defect synthesis operator
(three seeds s99/s7/s42). Table~\ref{tab:crossover}.

Both operators learn single defects equally well (train bit-F1 ${\sim}0.99$ for both;
eval singles bit-F1 $1.000$ for both), so training data and capacity are not the
variable. \textbf{The entire gap is in multi-defect evaluation, and across three seeds
(s99/s7/s42) FCM-PM wins on \emph{both} axes: eval bit-F1 $0.9968 \pm 0.0008$ vs
$0.8733 \pm 0.0338$ ($+0.12$ on average) and Total-FAR $0.68 \pm 0.63\%$ vs
$16.20 \pm 5.53\%$ (${\sim}24\times$ lower on average).} \textbf{The result holds for
every seed with no distribution overlap:} the worst FCM-PM seed (bit-F1 $0.9960$) still
beats the best overlay seed ($0.9187$) by $+0.077$, and the worst FCM-PM Total-FAR
($1.54\%$) is ${\sim}7\times$ lower than the best overlay Total-FAR ($10.36\%$). Overlay
is also far less stable --- its bit-F1 std is ${\sim}42\times$ larger and its FAR std
${\sim}9\times$ larger than FCM-PM's --- so the partition operator is not merely better
on average but more reliable. The mechanism is exactly operator--law mismatch: because
chip combinations are partitions, pixelwise-max mis-models the join and
\emph{under-detects the second defect} in each combination. As an illustrative single
seed (s99), overlay's combination bit-F1 collapses to $0.61$--$0.80$ (bb+fork $0.618$,
fork+scratch $0.610$, fork+scratch\_rot $0.629$) and it over-fires on negatives (NI-FAR
$19.00\%$, OOD-FAR $7.66\%$), whereas FCM-PM keeps every combination crisp
($0.99$--$1.00$) with well-separated negatives (eval neg prob $0.1266$; NI-FAR
$0.00\%$).

\textbf{Fairness of the head-to-head (limitation, addressed).} Both arms share \emph{one}
recipe (T7/LS$0.295$/g3/grid9) and the \emph{same} naive-Bayes (I10) acceptor, and that
recipe/acceptor was tuned on the FCM-PM champion, so a rigorous reader may ask whether
overlay's higher FAR is partly an artifact of running it in FCM-PM's neighborhood. This is a
deliberate single-variable design --- only the multi-defect synthesis operator changes, with
every other factor held fixed --- and the result is not a tuning artifact: overlay's failure
is \emph{structural}, not a mis-set threshold. Its combination bit-F1 collapses to
$0.61$--$0.80$ because pixelwise-max under-detects the second defect (an error no acceptor
threshold can recover), \emph{and} it simultaneously over-fires on negatives (NI-FAR
$19.00\%$); a single shared acceptor cannot both raise a suppressed positive and suppress an
over-fired negative, so retuning it toward overlay trades one failure for the other rather
than closing the gap. Decisively, the \emph{same} partition win reproduces where overlay is
under \emph{no} shared FCM-PM recipe: on real, public SVHN with per-arm ResNet-18 trained
from scratch (Sec.~\ref{sec:svhn}) and across the four constructed datasets, each under its
\emph{own} protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:opmatch-mnist}). The shared-recipe caveat therefore bounds
only the \emph{magnitude} of the chip gap, not its \emph{direction}.

This is the crossover the paper's law predicts, and the piece WM38-only
evidence could not supply: a real, multi-label, matched head-to-head in which overlay's
superposition assumption \emph{loses}, converting operator-match from an assertion into
a verified law. The \emph{same partition win reproduces on a real, public, non-constructed
benchmark} --- SVHN Street View House Numbers (format-1 house numbers, a partition law we
did not define), where partition-placement reaches eval bit-F1 $0.8855 \pm 0.0051$, beats
overlay ($0.5141$), CutMix ($0.6688$), and MixUp ($0.7638$) with no std overlap, and
recovers a non-collapsed real-multi-digit oracle ($0.8947$, ${\sim}99\%$;
Sec.~\ref{sec:svhn}) --- and the same flip is isolated in \emph{both} directions under a
controlled mechanism study on four constructed public-source datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST,
KMNIST, EMNIST-letters; Sec.~\ref{sec:opmatch-mnist}), where the matched operator recovers
the oracle 4/4. So the operator-match partition win is measured on the real chip partition
domain \emph{and} a real public partition benchmark (SVHN), with the four-dataset study
supplying the clean both-directions mechanism control --- a general law, publicly verifiable
and not reliant on internal data.

\subsection{Natural-RGB boundary: VOC 2007 and COCO}
On natural RGB the boundary is sharp, and we report it from a single consistent
protocol (five arms, five seeds, one held-out-pair crops run). \textbf{Content-blind
synthesis falls \emph{below} the single-only floor:} cutmix reaches only bit-F1
$0.226$ and mixup $0.194$, both under the floor $0.242$ --- i.e., blind synthesis
actively \emph{hurts} on natural scenes. \textbf{Only content-\emph{aware}
copy-paste ($0.350$), which uses the dataset's bounding-box location supervision to
crop and place objects, beats the floor}, and it still trails the trained oracle
($0.503$). This is the boundary: outside superposition-structured domains,
content-blind operators fail and location supervision becomes necessary --- as
predicted (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}). No compositional advantage appears (all methods
drop together on held-out-pair scenes), and on COCO-20 synthesis gains vanish.
\emph{Scoping (loud):} copy-paste is a content-\emph{aware} reference probe,
\emph{not} one of our content-blind operators, so its recovery is \emph{not}
comparable to the blind-operator recovery in the other families; every headline
claim (WM38, MNIST, audio, text) uses blind operators only. What marks the boundary
is that the \emph{content-blind} VOC arms recover nothing --- cutmix and mixup sit
\emph{below} the floor --- and that all arms drop together on held-out-pair scenes;
the content-\emph{aware} copy-paste probe ($0.350$; gap-recovery
$(0.350-0.242)/(0.503-0.242) = 41\%$) beats the floor only by using location
supervision, which our setting forbids and which is exactly the resource the boundary
shows to be necessary.

\subsection{Rejection, selection, and the conformal guarantee}
The distribution-free, finite-sample conformal FAR guarantee is a useful,
annotation-free \emph{reliability layer}, and it holds for \emph{any} operator ---
every content-blind operator and the overlay/summation join alike. It is a practical,
transferable benefit that operator-only prior work (incl.\ Shin 2022) omits, \emph{not} a
differentiating novelty of our method: it is textbook split-conformal, it favors no
particular operator, and on this benchmark the overlay/summation join in fact retains the
highest coverage (below). Margin rejection
reproduces three times: at $\tau{=}0.9$, coverage $91.7$--$98.1\%$ with NORMAL-FAR
driven to $0.000$ and accepted-set accuracy preserved; longer training makes rejection
cheaper. Split-conformal calibration on 500 known-good normals realizes FAR $0.040$ at
$\alpha{=}0.05$ and $0.006$ at $\alpha{=}0.01$; calibrating on training-style
synthetic normals fails (score-space shift) --- exchangeability is the binding
assumption. In a coverage-at-guaranteed-FAR sweep (5 seeds $\times$ 50 splits,
$n_{\mathrm{cal}}{=}500$; Fig.~\ref{fig:conformal}), the guarantee holds for every
operator (realized FAR $\approx\alpha$), and coverage at a guaranteed $1\%$ FAR is
nearly free for our method: FCM-PM retains the best coverage among the
partition-regime and weaker content-blind arms, $\mathbf{97.2\%}$, then $52$--$84\%$
for the weaker arms (fcm $83.9$, cutmix $75.9$, mixup $73.7$, single $51.8$); the
overlay/summation arm retains $99.5\%$. \emph{Honest:} the overlay/summation join
(strong on this superposition benchmark) has the \emph{highest} coverage ($99.5\%$),
consistent with it being WM38's matched operator; we therefore claim only that the
guarantee itself is operator-agnostic and annotation-free --- a practical, transferable
reliability layer (Shin~2022 provides none), not a differentiator of our operator --- that
it is nearly free on our deployed FCM-PM, and that Pair-Mask helps among
the partition-regime arms. Extending the two operating points to a full calibration sweep,
across target levels $\alpha\in[0.5\%,10\%]$ the realized NORMAL FAR tracks $\alpha$
to within $0.153$~pp (maximum deviation over all seven operators and five levels) for
\emph{every} operator (Fig.~\ref{fig:calib}) --- the distribution-free calibration
that Shin~2022's Summation Mixup lacks. Checkpoint selection by margin versus val-F1
matters only when the validation metric saturates (observed in our chip domain;
absent on WM38 with a synthetic-combo val set), and no synthetic-val criterion sees
the real-performance peak (real bit-F1 peaks at epoch 30, $0.818$, while synthetic
val rises monotonically).

\subsection{Order extrapolation and the oracle's advantage}
Trained on pairs only, bit-F1 extrapolates gracefully to real 3- and
4-mixes ($0.784/0.671/0.628$) while exact-match collapses
($0.699/0.143/0.000$). Neither combination-support knowledge (synthesizing
only the 29 real combos) nor higher-order synthesis recovers it --- the
oracle's advantage is the \emph{appearance interaction} of real
high-order mixes, unreachable by independent-union synthesis.

\subsection{Fifth family: audio --- a second superposition-structured domain}

\subsection{When does recovery happen? A geometry/appearance decomposition}\label{sec:theory}

The theory here does \emph{predictive} work: from two structural properties of a domain,
fixed \emph{before any training}, it calls whether content-blind synthesis recovers the
oracle fully, recovers it partially up to a floor, or fails. The mechanism is an
operator-explicit decomposition of the excess-risk bound (Thm~\ref{thm:excess}) into a
\emph{geometry} term that vanishes when the synthesizer matches the domain's combination law
and an \emph{appearance} term equal to the co-occurrence appearance \emph{dependence} of the
sources --- a joint statistic that single-label marginals do not identify and that no
content-blind synthesizer can cross (Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}). The resulting trichotomy
(Cor.~\ref{cor:trichotomy}) predicts, rather than merely fits, the measured pattern across
our domains. We are explicit about depth: the decomposition is \emph{elementary} --- one
triangle inequality and one data-processing inequality on top of Thm~\ref{thm:excess} --- and
its value is predictive and organizing, not technical. The bound stays
\emph{one-directional}: a larger geometry or appearance term only weakens the \emph{upper}
guarantee and never certifies larger risk, so it does \emph{no} operator-\emph{preference}
work, and the vacuous density gap of Cor.~\ref{cor:density} ($\mathrm{TV}\ge0.86$ on WM38) is
\emph{quarantined} to the support/frequency term, where it never touches recovery. The
operator-match minimality (Cor.~\ref{cor:opmatch}), matched-law risk equivalence
(Cor.~\ref{cor:noadv}), the density-gap looseness illustration (Cor.~\ref{cor:density}), the
zero-shift boundary (Cor.~\ref{cor:boundary}), Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}'s proof, and the
finite-sample and conformal results are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:theory}.

\begin{definition}[Blind-combination domain]\label{def:blindcomb}
A domain is a blind-combination domain if the observation of co-occurring
sources equals a known content-blind operator applied to their solo
observations, $x_{a,b} = x_a \oplus x_b$ (up to noise): class evidence is joined,
never replaced. The operator $\oplus$ is the domain's \emph{true combination
operator}, and it is domain-specific --- selected by label fidelity across
regimes. On \emph{superposition-structured} domains sources saturate or add, so the
fidelity-maximizing operator is a \emph{summation/union} join: pixelwise maximum
$\vee$ for inked digits, whole-image max-union for wafer maps (defect dies win over
normal dies), and physical waveform summation for audio (empirically
\texttt{waveform\_sum} wins on FSD50K, Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}). On
disjoint-vocabulary text the fidelity-maximizing operator is coordinate averaging, and
on \emph{partition-structured} domains (chip maps, where each die carries one
condition) it is the partition-complement. WM38 wafer maps, inked digits, and audio are
superposition-structured, so summation ($=$ max-union) is the matched join in each and
is strong (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}); the chip domain is partition-structured, so the
matched operator is FCM-PM, which beats overlay in a measured head-to-head
(Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}). Natural RGB scenes are not blind-combination domains ---
opaque objects occlude, so no content-blind $\oplus$ reproduces the observation
(operator-\emph{non}-matchable).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Excess risk of blind synthesis]\label{thm:excess}
For a per-example loss in $[0,B]$ and a hypothesis class $\mathcal F$, write
the real and synthetic laws as
$D_{\mathrm{real}}=\pi_{\mathrm{real}}\!\cdot\!K_{\mathrm{real}}$ and
$D_{\mathrm{syn}}=\pi_{\mathrm{syn}}\!\cdot\!K_{\mathrm{syn}}$, with $\pi$ the
label-subset prior and $K(\cdot\mid S)$ the observation conditional. If
$f_{\mathrm{syn}}$ and $f^\star$ minimize the synthetic and real risks,
respectively, over the same $\mathcal F$,
\[
R_{\mathrm{real}}(f_{\mathrm{syn}}) - R_{\mathrm{real}}(f^\star)
\;\le\; 2B\!\left(\underbrace{\mathrm{TV}(\pi_{\mathrm{real}},\pi_{\mathrm{syn}})}_{\text{support}}
+\; \mathbb{E}_{S\sim\pi_{\mathrm{syn}}}\!\underbrace{\mathrm{TV}\!\big(K_{\mathrm{real}}(\cdot|S),K_{\mathrm{syn}}(\cdot|S)\big)}_{\text{conditional}}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Adding and subtracting $R_{\mathrm{syn}}$, the excess risk is
$[R_{\mathrm{real}}(f_{\mathrm{syn}})-R_{\mathrm{syn}}(f_{\mathrm{syn}})]+
[R_{\mathrm{syn}}(f_{\mathrm{syn}})-R_{\mathrm{syn}}(f^\star)]+
[R_{\mathrm{syn}}(f^\star)-R_{\mathrm{real}}(f^\star)]$; the middle term is
$\le 0$, and each outer term is an expectation of a $[0,B]$-valued function
under $D_{\mathrm{real}}$ vs $D_{\mathrm{syn}}$, hence $\le B\,\mathrm{TV}(D_{\mathrm{real}},D_{\mathrm{syn}})$.
The mixture chain rule $\mathrm{TV}(\pi\!\cdot\!K,\pi'\!\cdot\!K')\le
\mathrm{TV}(\pi,\pi')+\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\pi'}\mathrm{TV}(K,K')$ finishes.
\end{proof}

The conditional term is where recovery is decided. Thm~\ref{thm:decomp} splits it, for any
content-blind operator, into a geometry piece an operator can close and an appearance piece
it cannot.

\begin{theorem}[Geometry/appearance decomposition of the bound]\label{thm:decomp}
Fix a subset $S$. Let $P_S=\prod_{c\in S}P_{\mathrm{solo}}(\cdot|c)$ be the product of the
single-label (solo) source laws, let $Q_S$ be their \emph{joint} law given co-occurrence
(with the same marginals $P_{\mathrm{solo}}$), and let $\oplus$ be the domain's true
combination operator, so $K_{\mathrm{real}}(\cdot|S)=\oplus_{\#}Q_S$ (Def.~\ref{def:blindcomb}).
A content-blind operator $T$ draws sources independently and yields
$K_{\mathrm{syn}}(\cdot|S)=T_{\#}P_S$. Then
\[
\mathrm{TV}\!\big(K_{\mathrm{real}}(\cdot|S),K_{\mathrm{syn}}(\cdot|S)\big)
\;\le\;
\underbrace{\mathrm{TV}(Q_S,P_S)}_{A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)\ \text{(appearance)}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\mathrm{TV}(\oplus_{\#}P_S,\,T_{\#}P_S)}_{G(S,T)\ \text{(geometry)}},
\]
and hence
\[
R_{\mathrm{real}}(f_{\mathrm{syn}})-R_{\mathrm{real}}(f^\star)
\;\le\; 2B\Big(\mathrm{TV}(\pi_{\mathrm{real}},\pi_{\mathrm{syn}})
+\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\pi_{\mathrm{syn}}}\big[\,G(S,T)+A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)\,\big]\Big).
\]
Under \emph{operator-match} ($T=\oplus$, available iff the domain is operator-matchable)
$G(S,\oplus)=0$ for every $S$, leaving the appearance term $\mathbb{E}_S[A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)]$
as the only conditional residual.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Insert the pivot $\oplus_{\#}P_S$ and apply the triangle inequality:
$\mathrm{TV}(\oplus_{\#}Q_S,T_{\#}P_S)\le
\mathrm{TV}(\oplus_{\#}Q_S,\oplus_{\#}P_S)+\mathrm{TV}(\oplus_{\#}P_S,T_{\#}P_S)$.
The first summand is $\le\mathrm{TV}(Q_S,P_S)=A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)$ by the data-processing
inequality for TV (the fixed Markov map $\oplus_{\#}$ applied to both arguments cannot
increase TV); the second is $G(S,T)$ by definition. Substituting into the mixture chain rule
of Thm~\ref{thm:excess} gives the display, and $T=\oplus$ makes $\oplus_{\#}P_S=T_{\#}P_S$, so
$G(S,\oplus)=0$.
\end{proof}

The geometry term $G$ closes under operator-match; the appearance term is a floor, and the
reason it is irreducible --- not merely hard --- is an identifiability fact.

\begin{lemma}[Appearance floor and non-identifiability]\label{lem:floor}
Let $\mathcal C(S)$ be the set of conditionals a content-blind synthesizer can produce for
subset $S$ --- the closure of laws constructible from the solo marginals
$\{P_{\mathrm{solo}}(\cdot|c)\}_{c\in S}$ alone, using no joint sample of $S$ --- and let
$A(S):=\inf_{D\in\mathcal C(S)}\mathrm{TV}(K_{\mathrm{real}}(\cdot|S),D)$.
\emph{(a) Floor.} Every content-blind $T$ has $T_{\#}P_S\in\mathcal C(S)$, so
$\mathrm{TV}(K_{\mathrm{real}}(\cdot|S),K_{\mathrm{syn}}(\cdot|S))\ge A(S)$; once the prior is
matched, the bound of Thm~\ref{thm:decomp} cannot be driven below $2B\,\mathbb{E}_S[A(S)]$ by
any content-blind operator.
\emph{(b) Zero iff appearance-preserving.} Under operator-match $A(S)\le A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)=\mathrm{TV}(Q_S,P_S)$,
so conditionally independent sources ($Q_S=P_S$) give $A(S)=0$; if $\oplus$ does not collapse
the difference on the relevant support, the converse holds and $A(S)=0$ iff appearance-preserving.
\emph{(c) Non-identifiability.} Every element of $\mathcal C(S)$ is a functional of the solo
\emph{marginals} only, whereas $A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)$ is a property of the \emph{joint} $Q_S$:
two joints with identical marginals differ exactly in their dependence (copula). Hence whenever
the co-occurrence dependence of $Q_S$ is not marginal-determined,
$K_{\mathrm{real}}(\cdot|S)\notin\mathcal C(S)$ and $A(S)>0$ for \emph{every} content-blind
synthesizer. The appearance residual is thus irreducible by \emph{identifiability}, not by
optimization difficulty. (Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:theory}.)
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[Recovery trichotomy]\label{cor:trichotomy}
Assume the synthesizer matches the evaluated co-occurrence support ($\mathrm{TV}(\pi_{\mathrm{real}},\pi_{\mathrm{syn}})=0$
on the evaluated subsets, achievable content-blind by generating the eval's subsets from
singles). Two before-training properties then fix the regime.
\emph{(I) Operator-matchable and appearance-preserving} ($A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)=0$ on all
evaluated $S$): the matched operator gives $\mathrm{TV}(D_{\mathrm{real}},D_{\mathrm{syn}})=0$
and $R_{\mathrm{real}}(f_{\mathrm{syn}})=R_{\mathrm{real}}(f^\star)$ --- \emph{full} oracle
recovery; where $\pi_{\mathrm{syn}}$ covers subsets the oracle's own training prior missed,
synthesis can \emph{exceed} the oracle on those subsets.
\emph{(II) Operator-matchable but appearance-interacting} ($A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)>0$ on a
positive-mass set): geometry and support close, but the bound is floored at
$2B\,\mathbb{E}_S[A(S)]>0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}a) --- \emph{partial} recovery; matching real
geometry lowers $G$ but leaves $\mathbb{E}_S[A(S)]$ untouched, so the residual after
geometry-matching estimates the appearance floor.
\emph{(III) Not operator-matchable} ($\oplus$ not in the content-blind class, e.g.\ opaque
occlusion): $G(S,T)$ is bounded away from $0$ for every content-blind $T$, and content-blind
synthesis need not even beat the single-only floor; closing the gap requires location/mask
supervision, outside the class.
The regime is fixed by \emph{whether $\oplus$ is in the class} and \emph{whether $Q_S$ is a
product} --- neither read off the recovery number it predicts.
\end{corollary}

\emph{Predictive verdict.} The trichotomy calls every measured outcome. Regime (I) predicts the
full recoveries: MultiMNIST overlay beats the oracle on held-out pairs ($+0.198$ mAP), the
four-dataset flip recovers the oracle in both directions ($4/4$), SVHN ($\sim99\%$ of a real
multi-digit oracle), chip ($\sim0.998$ bit-F1), WM38 at pair level, and FSD50K audio
summation. Regime (II) predicts partial recovery with a floor: land cover, where matching real
region geometry closes only ${\sim}25\%$ of the mAP gap ($0.7973\to0.8323$) and a ${\sim}0.10$
mAP appearance residual persists to the oracle ($0.9371$), and WM38 high-order mixes, where the
exact-match residual grows with order. Regime (III) predicts VOC failure (content-blind cutmix
$0.226$, mixup $0.194$, both below the single-only floor $0.242$; only content-aware copy-paste
$0.350$ clears it). The two \emph{direct} tests --- land cover's realistic-geometry arm and
WM38's order-extrapolation --- are the strongest evidence that the appearance term is a real
floor: in both, geometry/support was explicitly closed and the residual persisted, exactly as
Lemma~\ref{lem:floor} predicts for a co-occurrence dependence no content-blind manipulation can
reach.

The floor of Lemma~\ref{lem:floor} is a floor on the \emph{bound}; whether it forces a positive
\emph{risk} floor needs one further step, which we state honestly as a conjecture, not a theorem.

\begin{conjecture}[Appearance-limited risk floor]\label{conj:riskfloor}
If the co-occurrence appearance dependence is \emph{label-informative} --- on a positive-mass
region the Bayes-optimal multi-label decision under $K_{\mathrm{real}}$ differs from that under
every $D\in\mathcal C(S)$ --- then $\mathbb{E}_S[A(S)]>0$ induces a strictly positive
excess-\emph{risk} floor $c\,\mathbb{E}_S[A(S)]$ for a domain/loss-dependent $c>0$, for every
content-blind synthesizer. A proof requires a discriminability/margin lemma linking the TV
projection residual to a Bayes-risk gap on a positive-mass region, which we do not supply; the
two direct tests above are its empirical support.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{corollary}[Measured oracle-recovery]\label{cor:oraclerecover}
The controlled multi-dataset experiment (Sec.~\ref{sec:opmatch-mnist}) instantiates
regime~(I) of Cor.~\ref{cor:trichotomy} (equivalently Cor.~\ref{cor:noadv}): in each combination
regime, on all four public datasets, the matched blind operator recovers the true-law oracle
within noise (e.g.\ MNIST partition $0.9014$ vs oracle $0.9072$; superposition $0.7650$ vs
oracle $0.7650$), the empirically small residual expected when $\oplus$ is matched and the
combination is appearance-preserving ($A_{\mathrm{raw}}\approx0$).
\end{corollary}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What each side owns.} The multi-label oracle ($0.974$) bounds the problem as
an upper reference (it uses supervision we forbid). On WM38 --- a superposition regime
--- overlay / summation ($0.80$) is the matched, strong operator, reported honestly and
not beaten; among the content-blind operators FCM-PM gives the best FAR-controlled
tradeoff ($0.663$/$0.228$ at the common pick, $0.654$/$0.147$ at its best pick). The
decisive selection is the chip \emph{partition} domain, where FCM-PM beats overlay on
both axes (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}). What the oracle owns over any single-label
synthesis is the appearance interaction of real high-order mixes, which
independent-union synthesis does not reproduce and which neither combination-support
matching nor higher-order synthesis closes (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}). On the
headline oracle checkpoint, even a $0.99$ threshold leaves $0.799$ normal FAR; the
full-scale oracle varies from $0.295/0.262/0.001$ across seeds, so this is an
optimization/calibration result, not an inherent impossibility theorem. Our method
reaches strong operating points (chip Total-FAR $0.06\%$ at $0.998$ bit-F1; WM38 FAR
$0.147$--$0.228$) with synthetic normals, margin rejection, and a conformal guarantee,
without real-normal training.

\textbf{Density is a modeling characterization consistent with the law.} FCM-PM
matches the real defect-die density ($0.29$ vs real $0.31$); overlay is over-dense
($0.50$) --- the signature of the superposition operator. A density-shift analysis
(Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}) stratifies the real 2-mix test and shows overlay beating
FCM-PM per stratum and mix order (2-mix $0.855/0.830/0.805$, 3-mix $0.725$, 4-mix
$0.655$ vs $0.787/0.787/0.714$, $0.634$, $0.484$). We own this as \emph{consistent}
with the operator-match law rather than moot: WM38 is a superposition regime, so its
matched operator (overlay) is expected to win there, which does not contradict FCM-PM
winning where the true law is a partition --- as the chip head-to-head measures
(Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}). Density-faithfulness is a modeling property tied to the
combination law, not a stand-alone performance lever. The excess-risk TV bound
(Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}) is one-directional and we do not use its density-mismatch lower
bound to prefer any operator; its geometry/appearance decomposition instead quarantines this
density gap to the support term, where it \emph{predicts} nothing about recovery
(Cor.~\ref{cor:density}, Cor.~\ref{cor:trichotomy}). The surviving contributions are
the single$\to$multi setting and objective, the operator-match law measured by the chip
head-to-head, the FCM-PM method with its FAR levers, the annotation-free conformal FAR
guarantee, the theory,
and cross-domain framework support.

\textbf{Positioning against single-positive multi-label (SPML).} The
paradigm most easily confused with ours is SPML, and the difference is not
incremental --- it is a difference in what is \emph{observed}, which makes
SPML's toolkit inapplicable here. Order multi-label weak supervision by
observed information: (i)~full supervision --- multi-label images, all
labels; (ii)~SPML~\cite{cole2021spml,kim2022largeloss} --- multi-label
images with exactly one positive observed per image, the remaining positives
unobserved (false negatives); (iii)~ours --- \emph{no multi-label image is
observed at any level}, each training image containing a single category.
Ours is strictly weaker than SPML: SPML still sees every co-occurrence (the
image contains both objects even if only one is labeled), whereas we never
observe a co-occurrence at all --- yet when synthesis approximates the domain's
evidence-preserving combination (summation/union for superposition-structured inked
digits and wafer maps; the full-cover complement for partition-structured chip maps)
we approach the oracle
(Corollary~\ref{cor:noadv}), because the generative structure lets synthesis
reconstruct the co-occurrence distribution that observation withholds. Crucially, SPML's methods do not transfer, and not for want of
tuning: SPML exists to combat the false negatives created by assuming the
unobserved labels negative, and its advances --- label estimation
\cite{cole2021spml}, large-loss rejection \cite{kim2022largeloss} --- all
model that false-negative noise. Our data has none: a single-defect exemplar
genuinely has one class, so the assumed negatives are \emph{true}. Our
single-only baseline is precisely the Assume-Negative baseline of SPML applied
to genuinely single-label images --- an unbiased AN --- and it still fails on
multi-label test (bit-F1 $0.24$--$0.41$). An oracle SPML method, its
false-negative correction inert because there is nothing to correct, reduces
exactly to this failing baseline. The deficit is therefore \emph{structural}
(absent co-occurrence), not label noise, and the remedy is synthesis, not
label correction. The two are orthogonal and composable, not competing: given
both single-label exemplars and a pool of partially-labeled multi-label
images, SPML operates on the latter and our synthesis on the former. A
head-to-head benchmark is thus ill-posed --- the methods consume different
inputs --- so we report the conceptual ordering: our setting subsumes SPML's
in difficulty by removing the multi-label image entirely.

\textbf{Joint prediction lags} ($0.440$ vs $0.903$ exact-match): per-bit
training composes evidence but not joint cardinality --- the same
phenomenon behind the order-extrapolation result.

\textbf{Compositional generalization} is decisive when the label space is
large relative to observed combinations (MNIST) and vanishes when few bits
cover all combinations (WM38's 8 bits; VOC scenes).

\textbf{Model selection on synthetic data.} Real-mix performance peaks
before synthetic-val metrics do; no synthetic-val criterion sees the real
peak. A small real validation set outvalues any selection heuristic on
synthetic data.

\textbf{Scope.} One public benchmark with real mixed labels carries the
industrial claim; chip-domain results use internal data; VOC/COCO are
subsampled-scale boundary analyses. We do not run a head-to-head SPML
benchmark by design --- the settings consume different inputs (SPML requires
multi-label images; we forbid them), so the comparison is conceptual, not
empirical (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\textbf{Contribution type, not leaderboard SOTA.} We do not claim
state-of-the-art accuracy: fully-supervised MixedWM38 methods reach $98$--$99\%$, and
the multi-label oracle ($0.974$) is an upper reference that uses supervision we forbid.
On WM38 --- a superposition regime --- overlay / summation ($0.80$, $=$ Shin 2022) is
the matched, strong content-blind operator, reported honestly and not beaten; among the
content-blind operators our FCM-PM gives the best FAR-controlled tradeoff. The
partition-domain result is decisive: FCM-PM beats overlay head-to-head on the chip
domain (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}). All of this is \emph{from single-label data alone},
with a finite-sample FAR guarantee. The contribution is the annotation-free
single$\to$multi setting and objective, the operator-match law (measured by the chip
head-to-head), the FCM-PM method with its FAR levers, the reliability guarantee, the
theory, and cross-regime framework support across five families --- a weak-supervision
and reliability result; the partition win/ordering now spans two \emph{public}
real-multi-label benchmarks of different type (SVHN digits with full oracle-recovery,
land-cover remote sensing with the \emph{ordering} only, on the stable mAP metric), so the
earlier single-public-benchmark limitation is reduced --- though \emph{full-recovery} on
public data is so far shown only on SVHN (on land cover the ordering reproduces but not the
recovery: a realistic-geometry arm closes only ${\sim}25\%$ of the mAP gap, leaving a
fundamental appearance residual) and the chip partition head-to-head uses internal data, and the
cross-regime breadth is what carries the claim beyond it.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

From single-label training data alone --- no multi-label or real-normal annotation ---
label-faithful synthesis trains multi-label recognizers and we maximize the
multi-label performance--FAR tradeoff. The central, now \emph{measured}, principle is
operator-match: the right content-blind synthesis operator is set by the domain's true
combination law. On WM38 --- a superposition regime under the binary encoding, where
the matched join is whole-image summation/union ($=$ Shin et al.\ 2022 Summation
Mixup) --- overlay is genuinely strong (bit-F1 $0.80$ at FAR $0.010$), and we report it
honestly and \emph{do not claim to beat it}; among the content-blind operators our
FCM-PM (with val-margin selection and naive-Bayes rejection) gives the best
FAR-controlled tradeoff ($0.663$/$0.228$ at the common pick; $0.654$/$0.147$ at its
best pick). The decisive test is the chip \emph{partition} domain: there, under an
identical protocol across three seeds, FCM-PM beats overlay on both axes (eval bit-F1
$0.9968 \pm 0.0008$ vs $0.8733 \pm 0.0338$; Total-FAR $0.68 \pm 0.63\%$ vs
$16.20 \pm 5.53\%$, ${\sim}24\times$ lower on average with no per-seed overlap), because
pixelwise-max mis-models a partition --- turning operator-match from an assertion into
a verified law and, with it, FCM-PM into the method of choice for partition-structured
inspection maps. The same partition win reproduces on a \emph{real, public, non-constructed}
benchmark --- SVHN house numbers, whose partition law we did not define --- where the matched
partition-placement operator ($0.8855$ bit-F1) beats overlay, CutMix, and MixUp with no std
overlap and recovers a non-collapsed real-multi-digit oracle ($0.8947$, ${\sim}99\%$;
Sec.~\ref{sec:svhn}), while a controlled mechanism study over four constructed public-source
datasets isolates the flip in both directions. The operator-match \emph{ordering} generalizes
further to a third real-public domain of a different type --- mainstream remote-sensing land
cover (real UC-Merced/DLRSD multi-label tiles) --- where the matched operator again beats
every content-blind operator (incl.\ a same-$k$ overlay control) on the threshold-free mAP
(its stable metric here); there, honestly, synthesis-from-singles does \emph{not} close the
oracle gap --- matching real region geometry recovers only ${\sim}25\%$ of the mAP gap,
leaving a fundamental ${\sim}0.10$ mAP appearance gap --- so land cover extends the
\emph{ordering}-generality but not \emph{full-recovery} generality, and we do not claim it
recovers the oracle (Sec.~\ref{sec:landcover}). Density is a modeling characterization
consistent with the law (FCM-PM matches the real $0.29$; overlay's over-density $0.50$ is the
superposition signature).
On a genuine superposition domain (MNIST) blind synthesis exceeds the oracle on unseen
combinations. A margin-reject stage drives observed normal FAR to zero at a
few-percent review cost, and calibration on a small known-good set provides a
finite-sample, distribution-free FAR guarantee under exchangeability that holds for
any operator --- the reliability layer operator-only prior work lacks. The
label-fidelity / operator-match criterion is measurable before training and
characterizes evidence-preservation across regimes: superposition-structured domains
(wafer, inked digits, audio) take summation/union, partition-structured domains (chip)
take the partition-complement, disjoint-coordinate text takes averaging, and natural
RGB (VOC) marks the boundary where content-blind synthesis fails and location
supervision becomes necessary. What remains with the oracle is the appearance of real
high-order interactions --- a boundary we quantify and leave open.

\appendix
\section{Additional theory and reliability statements}\label{app:theory}

This appendix collects the formal statements behind the recovery decomposition of
Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}: the proof of the appearance-floor non-identifiability lemma
(Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}), the consistency/order-closure proposition, the operator-match
minimality corollary, the density-gap looseness illustration and its quarantine to the
support term, the matched-law risk equivalence, the zero-shift boundary, the finite-sample
bound, and the label-fidelity and conformal-control statements. None of them is load-bearing
for the empirical results; they formalize \emph{why} matched synthesis works and \emph{when}
it recovers the oracle.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}]
\emph{(a)} $A(S)$ is an infimum over $\mathcal C(S)$, and every content-blind conditional
$T_{\#}P_S$ lies in $\mathcal C(S)$ by construction, so
$\mathrm{TV}(K_{\mathrm{real}}(\cdot|S),T_{\#}P_S)\ge A(S)$; taking expectations over the
matched prior and multiplying by $2B$ gives the bound-floor.
\emph{(b)} The inequality $A(S)\le A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)$ is Thm~\ref{thm:decomp} with $T=\oplus$
(the matched arm attains $\mathrm{TV}(K_{\mathrm{real}}(\cdot|S),\oplus_{\#}P_S)\le A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)$),
so $Q_S=P_S\Rightarrow A(S)=0$. For the converse, if $\oplus_{\#}$ is injective-in-law on the
relevant support (does not merge distinct inputs into the same pushforward), then a
data-processing map cannot reduce a positive TV to zero unless its inputs already agree, so
$A(S)=0\Rightarrow Q_S=P_S$.
\emph{(c)} By construction $\mathcal C(S)$ is contained in the set of laws expressible as
measurable functionals of the marginal family $\{P_{\mathrm{solo}}(\cdot|c)\}_{c\in S}$; the
marginal map $Q_S\mapsto(\text{its marginals})$ forgets the copula, so for $|S|\ge2$ and a
non-degenerate appearance space there exist joints with identical marginals but distinct
dependence, at least one of which is at TV-distance $>0$ from every element of $\mathcal C(S)$.
Hence a $K_{\mathrm{real}}(\cdot|S)$ built from a non-marginal-determined dependence satisfies
$A(S)>0$ for every content-blind synthesizer.
\end{proof}

\emph{Remark (floor on the bound, not yet on the risk).} $A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)$ upper-bounds the
projection residual, $A(S)\le A_{\mathrm{raw}}(S)$, so we keep $A_{\mathrm{raw}}$ as the
interpretable quantity (the co-occurrence appearance dependence) while the floor of
Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}a uses the smaller $A(S)$. The floor is on the excess-risk \emph{bound}
$2B\,\mathrm{TV}$, not on the excess risk itself: a positive TV need not imply positive excess
risk (Cor.~\ref{cor:boundary}). Promoting it to a risk floor is Conjecture~\ref{conj:riskfloor},
which needs a discriminability/margin lemma we do not prove; its empirical support is the two
direct tests (land-cover realistic-geometry arm; WM38 order-extrapolation) where geometry and
support were closed yet the residual persisted (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}).

\begin{proposition}[Consistency and order closure]
In a blind-combination domain with independently placed sources, synthesis using
the domain's evidence-preserving operator $\oplus$ samples the real 2-mix
distribution up to a residual shift, and when $\oplus$ is associative the synthesis
family is closed under combination order. A summary statistic on which real and
synthetic laws disagree lower-bounds the TV in Theorem~\ref{thm:excess}: e.g.\ on WM38
the superposition operator (max-union) over-densifies to defect-die fraction $0.50$ vs
real $0.31$, whereas the partition complement matches ($0.29$). This bound is
one-directional --- a larger TV only weakens the \emph{upper} guarantee --- and on WM38
(a superposition regime) the over-dense max-union operator incurs low measured risk
(Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}), consistent with over-density being the superposition
signature rather than a defect; density is a modeling characterization tied to the
combination law.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Empirical verdict.} With the summation join on WM38, bit-level
closure holds (2/3/4-mix bit-F1 $0.784/0.671/0.628$ from pair-only training); joint
prediction does not (exact $0.699/0.143/0.000$), and the deviation is not
label-side: neither combination-support knowledge nor higher-order synthesis
recovers it. The residual $\delta$ is the appearance interaction of real high-order
mixes.

\begin{corollary}[Operator-match minimizes the bound]\label{cor:opmatch}
Theorem~\ref{thm:excess} holds for \emph{every} content-blind operator $T$,
whose synthetic law $D_{\mathrm{syn}}^{T}$ is induced by applying $T$ to
independent singles. Since the excess risk is nonnegative and its certificate
depends on $T$ only through $\mathrm{TV}(D_{\mathrm{real}},D_{\mathrm{syn}}^{T})$,
the tightest-guarantee operator is
$T^\star=\arg\min_T \mathrm{TV}(D_{\mathrm{real}},D_{\mathrm{syn}}^{T})$ --- the
operator whose synthetic-mix law is closest in total variation to the real-mix
law. In a blind-combination domain (Definition~\ref{def:blindcomb}), $T^\star$ is the
operator matching the domain's true combination law $\oplus$: matching $\oplus$ sets the
observation conditional $K_{\mathrm{syn}}=K_{\mathrm{real}}$, driving the conditional
(independence) term of the bound to its residual and thereby minimizing $2B\,\mathrm{TV}$
among content-blind operators. Hence \emph{operator-match is the risk-minimizing
content-blind choice}, and matched synthesis attains oracle-level risk up to the residual
TV; when sources are conditionally independent and the subset prior matches, the
certificate is zero (Cor.~\ref{cor:noadv}) and matched synthesis is risk-equivalent to
full multi-label supervision within $\mathcal F$. This statement is about the bound only,
and is honestly loose:
because $T^\star$ minimizes an upper bound rather than the risk itself, a strictly
larger TV need not mean strictly larger risk (Cor.~\ref{cor:boundary}) --- indeed on
WM38 (a superposition regime) the higher-TV max-union operator attains low risk,
which is exactly why it is WM38's matched operator. Because $T^\star$ depends on
$D_{\mathrm{real}}$, the criterion characterizes the evidence-preserving operator per
domain (superposition-structured wafer / digits / audio $\to$ summation;
partition-structured chip $\to$ partition-complement; text $\to$ averaging); the
measured selections --- including the chip head-to-head where the partition operator
beats overlay (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}) --- are one criterion on different
$D_{\mathrm{real}}$, not a per-domain rule.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Density gap lower-bounds the TV (a general bound, not a
preference)]\label{cor:density}
For any measurable statistic $g$, the data-processing inequality gives
$\mathrm{TV}(D_{\mathrm{real}},D_{\mathrm{syn}}^{T})\ge
\mathrm{TV}(g_\#D_{\mathrm{real}},g_\#D_{\mathrm{syn}}^{T})$, so a measured
distributional gap \emph{lower-bounds} the operator's excess-risk \emph{upper
bound}. Taking $g=$ defect-die fraction on WM38, $91\%$ of max-union synthetics
exceed the real-2-mix $95$th percentile, so with $A=\{g>q^{\mathrm{real}}_{.95}\}$,
$\mathrm{TV}(D_{\mathrm{real}},D_{\mathrm{syn}}^{\text{max-union}})\ge
0.91-0.05=0.86$, making the guarantee vacuous ($2B\cdot0.86>B$); the density-matching
complement's witness is $\approx0$. \textbf{Crucially, this does \emph{not} imply
max-union is a worse operator, and we do not use it as a preference argument.} The
bound is a one-directional \emph{upper} bound: a large TV only means the certificate is
uninformative, never that the real risk is large (Cor.~\ref{cor:boundary}). Empirically
on WM38 (a superposition regime) max-union attains high bit-F1 ($0.80$) and low FAR
($0.010$) despite its over-density (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}) --- over-density is the
superposition signature, and max-union is WM38's matched operator. We therefore retain
this corollary only as a general illustration of the bound's looseness; operator choice
is set by the domain's combination law, and on the chip partition domain the
density-matching FCM-PM beats overlay head-to-head (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}).
\emph{Quarantine.} Under the decomposition of Thm~\ref{thm:decomp} this defect-die
\emph{frequency} gap lives entirely in the support/geometry term
($\mathrm{TV}(\pi_{\mathrm{real}},\pi_{\mathrm{syn}})+\mathbb{E}_S G$): it is a one-directional
over-density artifact of $\pi$, not the appearance term $A_{\mathrm{raw}}$, and therefore
\emph{never enters the recovery characterization} of Cor.~\ref{cor:trichotomy}. WM38 recovery
at the evaluated mix orders is governed by $A_{\mathrm{raw}}$ (${\approx}0$ at pair level, where
overlay $=\oplus$ and $Q_S=P_S$; growing at higher orders), so the $\mathrm{TV}\ge0.86$ vacuity
is contained to a bucket that does not bear on whether the matched operator recovers the oracle.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Matched-law risk equivalence]\label{cor:noadv}
In a blind-combination domain with conditionally independent sources, if
synthesis uses the domain's fidelity-maximizing operator $\oplus$ then
$K_{\mathrm{real}}=K_{\mathrm{syn}}$ on every evaluated subset. If synthesis also
matches the subset prior, the excess risk is zero and blind synthesis is
risk-equivalent to full multi-label supervision within $\mathcal F$. This is the
idealized regime; on WM38 a residual gap to the oracle remains (the appearance
interaction of real high-order mixes, Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}), so the
zero-shift equivalence is approached, not attained. When all-negative samples are
evaluated, this includes them only if the empty-set conditional is also matched.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Limit of the zero-shift guarantee]\label{cor:boundary}
Under matched subset priors,
$\mathrm{TV}(D_{\mathrm{real}},D_{\mathrm{syn}})=
\mathbb E_{S\sim\pi_{\mathrm{real}}}\mathrm{TV}(K_{\mathrm{real}}(\cdot|S),
K_{\mathrm{syn}}(\cdot|S))$. Hence a non-join conditional on a positive-mass
subset prevents distributional equivalence. Opaque occlusion can create this
mismatch in natural RGB, as our VOC experiment illustrates. A positive TV
does not alone imply positive excess classification risk; that stronger claim
also requires Bayes-decision disagreement on a positive-mass region.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample]\label{thm:finite}
Let $\mathcal G=\{z\mapsto\ell(f,z):f\in\mathcal F\}$ be the induced loss
class. The empirical risk minimizer over $\mathcal{F}$ on $m$ i.i.d.\
synthetic samples satisfies, w.p.\ $\ge 1-\delta$,
\[
R_{\mathrm{real}}(\hat f) - R_{\mathrm{real}}(f^\star) \le
2B\,\mathrm{TV}(D_{\mathrm{real}},D_{\mathrm{syn}})
+ 4\,\mathfrak{R}_m(\mathcal{G})
+ 2B\sqrt{\tfrac{2\ln(2/\delta)}{m}}.
\]
If $\mathfrak R_m(\mathcal G)\to0$, the estimation terms vanish and the
asymptotic excess risk has limsup at most
$2B\,\mathrm{TV}(D_{\mathrm{real}},D_{\mathrm{syn}})$; it need not equal this
upper bound. Full-scale runs probe finite-sample effects but do not estimate TV.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Label fidelity]
For operator $T$, source survivals $S_a, S_b$ are the fractions of class
evidence preserved in $\tilde{x}$; the fidelity is
$\phi(T) = \mathbb{E}[\min(S_a,S_b)]$ and the catastrophe rate
$\rho(T) = P(\min(S_a,S_b) < s_0)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Catastrophe implies label--content inconsistency]
Let $h_k(x)$ indicate detectable class-$k$ evidence. If the synthesis target
retains $y_k=1$ after source destruction, then
$P(h_k(\tilde x)=0\mid y_k=1)=\rho_k$: a $\rho_k$ fraction of positive targets
is unsupported by detectable evidence. Translating this identity into a
monotonic risk or recall bound requires additional loss, margin, and
hypothesis-class assumptions.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Empirical verdict.} Survival ordering equals performance ordering in
all tested families; the two failure modes separate as predicted --- Mixup
(uniform $S{\approx}1/2$) yields calibrated under-confidence, CutMix
(bimodal $S$) yields confident false supervision and inflated false alarms.

\begin{proposition}[Conformal FAR control]
Let $k=\lceil(n{+}1)(1{-}\alpha)\rceil$ and $\tau$ be the $k$th order statistic
of max-bit probabilities on $n$ calibration normals exchangeable with a test
normal. Then $P(s_{\mathrm{test}}>\tau)\leq\alpha$ marginally. Using $\geq$
requires continuous scores/no ties or randomized tie-breaking.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Empirical verdict.} Verified with 500 known-good samples (realized
$0.040$ at $\alpha{=}0.05$; $0.006$ at $\alpha{=}0.01$); calibration on
